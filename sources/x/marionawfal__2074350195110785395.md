🇺🇸🇮🇷 Oil at $70 is telling you a story the tankers don't support, and the gap between them is where the next shock is hiding.

The price says the crisis passed. The physical flow says otherwise. 

Hormuz transits are still running at a fraction of the prewar baseline, Iran turned back six ships in a single morning and rerouted an entire convoy into its own northern lane, and large crude carriers have gone days without entering the Gulf at all. 

No tankers in means no oil out.

So what's holding prices down? Buffers. 

The U.S. has drained its stockpiles to levels last seen in 2004, releasing from the strategic reserve for months. 

China cut imports by three million barrels a day partly by eating its own reserves. 

The world has been living off savings and calling it supply.

That works exactly as long as the savings last. Emergency stockpiles suppress the price signal without fixing the shipping problem, which means the market has stopped pricing the actual strait and started pricing the cushion. 

When the cushion thins, and reserves are finite by definition, the unsolved physical crisis reappears all at once, with less buffer left to absorb it.

The shock wasn't cancelled. It was postponed, and the postponement is running out.
