🇺🇸🇮🇷 Trump threatened to unleash the “gates of hell” against Iran, then he backed away, which in Washington may have been seen as restraint.

According to Geopolitical analyst Pepe Escobar, Tehran interpreted it very differently.

They saw it as proof that Iran’s deterrence had worked, America’s military options were narrowing, and that the longer Iran holds out, the more leverage it gains.

Pepe says that transformed Iran’s objectives.

Tehran is no longer thinking only about surviving the war, lifting sanctions or securing control over the Strait of Hormuz.

According to his sources, forcing the U.S to retreat from West Asia has now become a central strategic goal.

Iran began this conflict trying to prevent America from imposing its will.

Pepe believes it now sees an opportunity to dismantle the military system that allowed Washington to threaten it in the first place.

And Tehran reportedly believes it has found the mechanism.

He says Iran has replaced its previous proportional retaliation policy with a new warning: 

Strike one Iranian energy target, and Iran will strike ten targets across America’s regional network.

The message to Gulf governments is brutal.

If they help Trump attack Iran, their refineries, pipelines and export facilities will pay the price.

Pepe says that threat is one reason regional leaders pushed Trump to stand down.

Saudi Arabia, in particular, understands that another major escalation could devastate the infrastructure it depends on to export oil.

For decades, American bases were supposed to protect Gulf allies from Iran, but now those bases are putting the countries hosting them at risk.
