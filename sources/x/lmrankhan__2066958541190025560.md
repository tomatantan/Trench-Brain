What I like about building on top of Polymarket and Hyperliquid is composability.

These platforms will naturally go after the lowhanging fruit, and they should. This pushes new founders to explore opportunities on the fringes.

There are hundreds of products that can be built on top of these ecosystems that extend their liquidity and network effects while serving entirely different end users. Many of these opportunities are niche enough that they're difficult for the core teams to prioritize, creating room for startups with deep domain expertise.

This is one of the advantages of open ecosystems. Hyperliquid and Polymarket benefit from an expanding surface area of products, while new founders can move faster, experiment more aggressively, and out innovate centralized competitors over the long run by leveraging composability.

Three startups from our current batch that I'm excited about:

ETFs for prediction markets @ito_markets

Options for prediction markets @convallax

Lending for prediction markets @ValkriLabs

What stands out is that all three teams have deep domain expertise. They're building products that they themselves would use, which is often the best indicator that they've identified a real customer need.

All three products are live in beta, growing week over week, and have insights that I haven't seen elsewhere in the market.

We'll continue to see players like Kalshi try to own the entire user experience, but I think there's a ton of value in the open ecosystem approach. If prediction markets become a major asset class, a meaningful amount of the value creation won't happen at the exchange layer. It will happen in the products built on top of it.
