High modernism as described by the book Seeing Like A State is the muscular faith of the 19th and early 20th centuries: linear progress, scientific law, technical mastery, rational production, and the belief that nature, including human nature, can be redesigned for greater welfare. 

In its own historical moment this was not obviously insane. Railroads, electricity, metallurgy, medicine, public health, chemistry, mass production, canals, and sanitation had changed life so violently that awe was rational.

High modernism was not only domination. It also “promised freedom from scarcity, want and the arbitrariness of natural calamity.”

Seeing Like A State's author was right about the danger of high modernism: when the map can no longer be corrected by the territory, muscular faith becomes catastrophe. But we overlearned the warning.

Bring back high-modernist ambition, and add feedback loops. Abundance via superintelligence can be a powerful force making the feedback real. 

We must dream and build again.
