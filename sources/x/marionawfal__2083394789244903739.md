🇸🇩 Sudan’s war survives because outsiders keep feeding it

The Rapid Support Forces (government-created paramilitary force) should have been exhausted by now.

Yet they continue to receive weapons, fuel, training and foreign fighters through a logistics corridor that runs across eastern Libya under Khalifa Haftar’s forces.

Investigations have mapped the camps and supply routes, often using the fighters’ own social media posts, and the UAE sits at the center of that network.

Sudanese gold moves out while money, arms and support move in, with agricultural leases and long term interest in Red Sea access forming part of the same calculation.

Egypt has chosen the other side and backs the Sudanese army, while Iran maintains links to Islamist factions fighting alongside it and Turkey holds a smaller position.

Each external actor is pursuing its own interests in resources, ports or regional influence, and the Sudanese people are left with the consequences.

Cities have been emptied.

Famine has spread.

Atrocities have been documented on a scale that should have forced a response, yet the flow of external support continues because it remains useful to the powers providing it.

A war that could have ended through exhaustion is instead being prolonged by design.

As long as the supply lines stay open and the outside interests stay engaged, the fighting does not stop.
