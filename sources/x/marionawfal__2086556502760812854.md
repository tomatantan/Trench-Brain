Trump launched a war of choice on February 28, overestimating American power to bend Tehran and underestimating Iran’s capacity to survive and harden.

Hard power has now crowded out whatever pragmatism remained.

Former IRGC and security officials are calling the shots on three core principles: time is on their side, they can still escalate, and they must lock in a new political and economic reality in the Gulf.

That reality is control of the Straits.

Miller sees no balance of interests that could produce a durable deal — Iran’s requirements and Trump’s simply do not overlap.

So the administration has fallen back on the Goldilocks strategy: not too hot, not too cold.

Keep the kinetic and economic pressure on, hope the Iranian economy cracks, and leave a diplomatic door ajar.

Miller’s sharpest warning: the regime is not brittle.

No fractures among the security elites, no organized opposition ready to capitalize.

Economic pressure so far has only emboldened them, new demands now link Palestine, Lebanon and Yemen.
