🇸🇦🇹🇷🇵🇰 Prof. Glenn Diesen says the new Mecca defense pact shouldn't be mistaken for a Middle Eastern version of NATO.

The agreement may contain an Article 5-style mutual defense clause, but that doesn't automatically commit members to military intervention. Like NATO itself, each country ultimately decides how, or whether, to respond to an attack.

Diesen believes the alliance is held together by different strategic interests. Saudi Arabia is seeking stronger security guarantees as confidence in U.S. protection weakens, Pakistan wants to preserve its longstanding ties with Riyadh without sacrificing relations with Iran, while Turkey has little interest in seeing Tehran weakened enough to leave itself as Israel's next primary regional rival.

"An attack on one should be considered an attack on all... but it doesn't obligate everyone to retaliate."
