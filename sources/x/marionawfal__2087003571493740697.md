🇺🇸🇹🇷 Trump slipped off Air Force One in Turkey inside a catering truck, and the White House spent the next month telling the country he was on the plane...

Start with the part that holds up. 

Intelligence flagged a credible Iranian threat against the president, Ankara sits on Iran's border, American strikes had resumed the night before, and the people responsible for keeping him alive put him on the least predictable aircraft available. That call looks right.

What follows is the harder conversation. 

The administration did not withhold his location. It supplied one. 

The official account has not changed since July 8, and the night he landed in Britain the White House posted a photo of the legacy jumbo jet and said he arrived aboard it. 

The press pool filed a report describing an uneventful flight from a cabin the president had never entered.

On September 11, 2001, Bush's team said only that he was at an undisclosed location, then confirmed the real one within hours. 

Ronald Rowe, who ran the Secret Service, put the standard plainly: Americans should know what their president is doing daily while the methods protecting him stay out of view. 

A catering truck is a method. A destination is a fact.

So why keep the story running weeks after the wheels were down? 

Not to spare anyone embarrassment, because the president says the dangerous part on the record. 

Which leaves the possibilities that matter. 

Operational habit that nobody thought to lift once the danger passed, or a communications shop that had spent weeks defending a gifted Qatari jet missing some of the old plane's defenses, subpoenaing reporters over that very reporting, and could not stomach one more headline about a presidential aircraft.
