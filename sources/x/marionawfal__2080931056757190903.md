🇺🇸🇮🇷 The U.S. is desperately trying to prevent Iran from rebuilding its nuclear programme while needing to get it back to the negotiating table to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. 

The problem is that one of Iran's most important remaining nuclear facilities is believed to be buried too deep for even America's most powerful conventional bunker-buster bombs to reliably destroy.

That has led some analysts to discuss a possibility that until recently would have been almost unthinkable: a U.S. tactical nuclear strike.

The greatest danger wouldn't necessarily be Iran's immediate retaliation.

It would be breaking an almost 80-year taboo on the use of nuclear weapons.

That precedent wouldn't stay in the Middle East.

Russia has repeatedly threatened nuclear escalation during the war in Ukraine but has so far stopped short of crossing that line. 

Part of that restraint comes from the enormous political cost of being the first country since 1945 to use a nuclear weapon in war.

If the U.S crosses that line first, it's easier for others to follow.

It becomes far harder for Washington and its allies to argue that Russia must never do something they have already done themselves.

A tactical nuclear strike might solve one military problem in Iran.

But it could also make nuclear weapons politically usable again in every future war that involves a rival with nuclear capabilities.
