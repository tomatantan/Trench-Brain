Uwe Boll, who wrote and directed Citizen Vigilante, thinks Europe's illegal immigration crisis is pushing society toward a far more dangerous breaking point than most people realise.

It's creating a generation of people who no longer believe the state is willing, or even able, to protect ordinary citizens.

Citizen Vigilante has been branded reckless, inflammatory, and dangerous because it tells the story of a man who watches gang rapists and violent criminals walk free, loses faith in the justice system, and decides to become judge, jury, and executioner himself.

Germany banned it, but Uwe uploaded it to X and @elonmusk posted about it, helping it to reach over 25 million views.

Uwe says people are obsessing over the wrong thing. The scary part isn't that Citizen Vigilante exists. It's how many people watched it and thought, "I understand exactly why he snapped."

He points to gang rapes, knife attacks, grooming scandals, and violent crimes that governments have spent years downplaying while ordinary people are told they're the problem for noticing them.

Illegal immigration isn't a stand-alone issue; it's the spark that's exposing something much bigger: a justice system people increasingly believe protects offenders more than victims.

A political class more interested in policing language than restoring public confidence, and a society poisoned with suicidal empathy.

That's why Uwe says Citizen Vigilante should be seen as a warning, not a blueprint.

He's not arguing that vigilantes are the answer.

He's arguing that if governments keep refusing to confront the problems people are living with every day, eventually some will stop believing democracy itself is capable of delivering justice.

When citizens lose faith that the state will enforce the law fairly, they don't simply become more cynical; they begin looking elsewhere for justice.

And once a society starts losing that kind of trust, getting it back is far harder than controlling a border.
