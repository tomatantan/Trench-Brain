🇸🇾🇮🇱 There was a live crisis over a secret Syrian nuclear site that almost nobody knew about, and it just got resolved quietly.

Axios' @BarakRavid, breaking the story:

"Very few people in the U.S., Israel and Syria were aware of this."

The site, known as "Site 99," held leftover material from Assad's Al-Kibar reactor program, the one Israel bombed back in 2007. 

Not weapons-grade, but usable in a dirty bomb. 

Israel had already hit the site's entrances after Assad fell to keep it sealed, and made clear to Washington it would bomb it again if the material wasn't removed.

For over a year this ran on a quiet parallel track: NSC and the Israeli PM's office working it directly, Israel monitoring for any sign Syria's new government was trying to access the material, at one point even suspecting Turkey was involved. 

When Washington first raised it with Damascus, Syrian officials reportedly didn't even know the material was there.

The resolution: a signed agreement between the IAEA and Syria, three weeks ago, to remove it. Not yet done, still an active operation, but the closest thing to a crisis nobody had to escalate.

Worth remembering next time someone says nothing changed on the ground after Assad fell.
