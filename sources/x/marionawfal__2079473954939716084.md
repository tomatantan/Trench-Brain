🇺🇸🇨🇳 Hasan Piker has once again sparked backlash after praising Mao Zedong.

Mao's policies are widely associated with the Great Chinese Famine, one of the deadliest disasters in history, in which tens of millions of people died.

The bigger story, though, isn't Hasan.

It's that the internet increasingly rewards the loudest, most provocative voices over the most thoughtful ones.

Before social media, and especially before AI-driven recommendation algorithms, politics was slower. Today, attention is the currency.

The people who dominate the conversation aren't usually the most nuanced; they're the ones willing to be the most outrageous. Outrage spreads faster than restraint, and algorithms know it.

That creates a powerful incentive to become more extreme over time, because being ignored is often worse than being controversial.

Until that incentive changes, don't be surprised if politics keeps swinging between louder and louder extremes.
