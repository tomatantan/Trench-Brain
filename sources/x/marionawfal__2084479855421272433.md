🚨🇺🇸 Anduril is in advanced talks to build drone boats at Baltimore's historic Sparrows Point yard, once home to Bethlehem Steel...

-The defense-tech company has signed a memorandum of understanding with the operators of the 3,300-acre site, and its investment in building out part of it for unmanned surface vessel production could run into the hundreds of millions

-Maryland Gov. Wes Moore's office has been involved, and any deal would likely come with state incentives, though no lease is signed and talks could still fall apart

-The location matters, putting boat production near Washington and the Coast Guard's largest shipyard for easier testing and fleet integration

-The war made the case for the product: American drone boats built by Saronic rescued two Apache crew members after their helicopter was shot down and struck Iran's shipping and submarine infrastructure, though surveillance remains their main job

-Anduril, valued at $61 billion after a spring funding round, is pushing to roughly double its weapons production capacity, with partnerships in the UK and South Korea

-The technology is still rough, with test boats misidentifying objects, crashing, drifting off or stalling, and the Navy struggling to make the piloting software reliable in open water

A steel mill that built America's largest cargo ship in 1956 becoming a factory for crewless attack boats is about as clean a picture of the shift as you get.

What sells them is the same math running the Gulf right now, where a $4 million interceptor gets spent on a cheap drone, and both sides are learning that quantity has a quality all its own.
