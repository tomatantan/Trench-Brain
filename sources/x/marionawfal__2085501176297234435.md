🇺🇸🇸🇦 For the first time since 1985, the U.S. bought zero Saudi oil in a month.

July went by without a single barrel of Saudi crude reaching US refiners, a full-month first in 41 years.

The chart tells the whole arc. Imports actually peaked above 600,000 barrels a day in April, then fell off a cliff through May and June and flatlined at zero by July.

The cause is the war. Iran's disruption of the Strait of Hormuz has choked Gulf exports, so US refiners went hunting elsewhere and leaned on other suppliers, including Venezuela, now that sanctions there have eased.

It's not permanent. Kpler expects Saudi flows to bounce back to around 300,000 barrels a day this month.

Still, a clean zero from a country the U.S. built decades of Middle East policy around shows just how much the war is redrawing the oil map.
