Philip Pilkington ran the numbers. 

Around 150 ships a day moved through Hormuz before the war. 

Five months of closure left a hole of THOUSANDS of transits, and today's trickle of a dozen-odd ships doesn't begin to fill it. 

You can print money, he kept repeating, but you can't print oil.

Which is why he thinks the cheerful price on your screen is a mirage.

In his world, energy traders look at that gap the way you'd look at a magic trick you've already figured out.

His most provocative guess, offered as exactly that, is that China is quietly draining its massive reserves to cover America's shortfall through the 60-day window, and won't do it forever.
