This is the wrong answer.

But I fear the industrial logic points in this direction, as @deanwball pointed out last week. The fact pattern echoes EVs or solar panels; the US is afraid domestic investment will plummet if Chinese open source tech is allowed to fully disseminate and that may cause the US to lose its lead.

The best solution, IMO, is to let competition be fully open and, if necessary, give government grants to subsidize frontier research if we see capital markets falter. (Delay true nationalization as long as possible, but I fear it may be inevitable in the long run.)

Second best solution is, if you must ban, is to just ban inference. I.e., don't let American companies buy inference from Chinese labs, but they CAN use the models and serve them from American inference providers. This is akin to what happens in China, where American companies must partner with Chinese companies to sell goods in China.

A full-on ban of Chinese models is absolutely the worst of all worlds.
