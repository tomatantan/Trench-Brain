"Everything was going swimmingly, it seemed like the community was on board until they weren't. Out of nowhere there was a lot of opposition, and we traced exactly where it was coming from"

Sam Lyman on how a single out-of-town activist backed by Greenpeace stirred up opposition to a Texas data center

"When I was at Raya a few years ago, we were building a data center in Corsicana, Texas. It seemed like the community was on board until suddenly there was a lot of Facebook opposition. We traced it to an environmental activist who didn't even live in the city, she came from the city next door. She had partnered with Greenpeace, which at the time had received a huge infusion of cash from a rival cryptocurrency to Bitcoin, whose explicit purpose was to cast fear, uncertainty, and doubt on Bitcoin itself"

"That's when I realized we need to follow the money, and I translated that ethos to the national project we're doing now. What I'm seeing on a national scale is exactly what I saw back then. A couple of committed anti data center activists online gin up the community, hold rallies and petitions, and get hundreds of people to a town hall that typically only has four to five people"

"All of a sudden you have town council members seeing hundreds of people at their meetings, lines out the door just to protest a data center project. It's unprecedented for what these council members have seen. They interpret it as mass opposition, and oftentimes they vote accordingly"
