🇺🇸 Intel's problem was never cash. It was that the spreadsheet guys took the wheel from the engineers.

Former CEO Pat Gelsinger says the company spent roughly $100 billion on dividends and buybacks while its factories rotted.

It hadn't built a new fab in a decade and skipped buying the cutting-edge EUV machines that spreadsheet math flagged as bad investments, but that any technologist knew were the whole ballgame.

Once the bean counters replaced the builders, business leaders promoted more business leaders, and the engineering edge that made Intel valuable in the first place eroded. 

You can't run a chip company through a spreadsheet because the calls that matter only pay off if you understand where the technology is going.
