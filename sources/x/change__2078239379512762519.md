"Residents deserve real information and a real say before one is built next to their homes, not after."

A data center broke ground in North Richland Hills, Texas, in May without a single public hearing because the site had been zoned for industrial use since 1985, long before nearby neighborhoods were built.

Now, another company is proposing a second data center in the city, one nearly the size of six football fields, even as North Richland Hills Mayor Jack McCarty has said he doesn't want data centers near residential areas.

Nearly 20,000 people have signed a resident-led petition calling on the city to require public hearings and buffer zones before this, or any future data center, is approved.
