Trump's flat 10% tariff on the world expires Friday, and what replaces it splits countries into two tiers.

Right now nearly every country pays that same 10% to sell into America.

It was only ever a patch. The Supreme Court struck down his original tariffs back in February.

He replaced them with an emergency law that came with a 150 day timer, and it runs out on Friday.

What follows is a forced labour tariff across 60 economies, and this one isn't equal.

14 countries pay 10%. The other 46 pay 12.5%.

The cheaper tier is mostly the ones who already cut deals with Washington. Canada, Mexico, the UK, the EU, Taiwan.

Everyone else sits at 12.5%. China, Japan, India, South Korea, Brazil, Switzerland.

Two more are already locked in outside all of that. Brazil takes 25% tomorrow, and Canada takes 50% on $20 BILLION of goods next month.

The Canada one runs on a 1930 law nobody has used once in 96 years.

None of it touches what's already in place either. Steel, aluminium and copper stay at 50%, cars and chips at 25%, Chinese EVs at 100%.

Brazil ends up worst hit. The 12.5% stacks on tomorrow's 25% and takes them to 37.5%.

Nothing was repealed in February. It just got rebuilt out of older laws.
