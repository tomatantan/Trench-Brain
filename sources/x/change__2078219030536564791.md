"These systems put all of us under constant surveillance."

That's how a new petition signed by over 17,000 people describes automated license plate readers, the pole-mounted cameras used by police departments and private companies to scan and record every passing vehicle.

The fight is already playing out in court. A Virginia lawsuit is testing whether Norfolk’s citywide Flock camera network went too far. Residents there, backed by the Institute for Justice, have appealed to the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals after a federal judge sided with the city in January, arguing that the surveillance amounts to warrantless searches under the Fourth Amendment.
