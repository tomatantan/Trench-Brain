🇵🇸🇱🇧🇮🇱 October 7 was horrifying enough, but secret documents suggest Hamas expected something even worse.

A multi-front war designed to pull Israel apart from every direction.

Newly revealed Hamas documents, reported by Doron Kadosh of Israel Army Radio, show years of secret coordination with Hezbollah and Iran.

Letters, meetings, intelligence sharing, operational planning, and one giant assumption from Yahya Sinwar:

When the big war came, Hezbollah would join.

In 2021, during Operation Guardian of the Walls, Hamas, Hezbollah, and Iran’s Revolutionary Guards reportedly operated a joint intelligence room in Beirut.

Hezbollah helped Hamas track Israeli military movements, it even warned Hamas about Israel’s “Metro” deception operation roughly two hours before it began, helping spoil one of Israel’s most ambitious attempts to trap Hamas fighters underground.

That wasn’t symbolic support, it was battlefield coordination.

By 2022, Hamas wanted more.

Senior Hamas leaders met Nasrallah in Beirut, alongside an Iranian Revolutionary Guard commander, and argued that the moment was ripe.

Israel was politically divided, the West was distracted by Ukraine, normalization with Arab states was advancing, and Hamas wanted to blow it all up.

Sinwar’s preferred plan reportedly had a name: “The Second Promise Campaign.”

A surprise attack from Gaza, Lebanon, Syria, and even Jordan, designed not to pressure Israel, but to destroy it.

Nasrallah listened, but he hesitated. His question was simple: what exactly is the goal?

By August 2023, Sinwar convinced himself the hesitation was gone.

He told Hamas leaders that Iran and Hezbollah were ready.

They weren’t.

At 6:29 a.m. on October 7, Hamas launched the massacre and begged Nasrallah to join, while apologizing for not notifying them in advance of the attack.

The message was desperate: Fire rockets, open a front in the north, send in the ground forces, but the Radwan invasion never came.

That may be one of the biggest reasons October 7, as catastrophic as it was, did not become something even larger.

The documents expose two truths at once.

First, Hamas did not stumble into October 7.

It planned for years, coordinated with allies, studied Israel’s divisions, and dreamed of a regional war.

Second, Sinwar miscalculated.

He believed the “axis of resistance” would move as one.

Instead, when the hour came, Hezbollah didn't.
