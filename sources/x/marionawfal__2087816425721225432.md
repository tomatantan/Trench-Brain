🇮🇷🇺🇸 The longer the Iran war drags on, the more damage it does to the U.S.

America can still dominate individual battles, but depleted munitions and stretched naval assets mean it's putting increasing pressure on its industrial capacity.

That is where China becomes dangerous; its advantage is not simply lower costs or better economics.

It is sheer industrial capacity, manpower, control over critical rare earths, and deep influence across global supply chains that become increasingly important in a prolonged war of attrition.

Russia and Iran also benefit from the same dynamic if enough of the world refuses to fully choose sides.

India has already shown how valuable that position can be, maintaining ties with Washington while also doing business across the China-Russia orbit.

In a world where oil supplies are uncertain and global trade is under pressure, more countries may decide that neutrality is simply more profitable than alignment.

That is the real danger for the U.S.

If Europe and other major economies fully backed Washington, restricted trade with Iran and its partners, and helped absorb the economic cost of keeping Hormuz open, Tehran would eventually face overwhelming pressure.

But if they stay on the sidelines, Iran gains time, China gains leverage, and Russia gains room to maneuver.

Neutrality sounds passive, but in a long war, it can decide who eventually wins.
