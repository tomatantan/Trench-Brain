🇺🇸🇳🇬 An American missionary pilot is finally coming home after almost 10 months in the hands of Sahel jihadists.

Kevin Rideout was grabbed from his house in Niamey last October, right next to the presidential palace, by gunmen linked to Islamic State’s local franchise.

He had spent nearly 20 years flying aid and mission flights across Niger.

The White House made his recovery a quiet priority, even softening some of its posture toward the junta governments in the region to keep intelligence flowing.

Now officials say he is in U.S. custody and heading back to the States.

The Sahel has become one of the hardest places on earth to get hostages out of.

Groups tied to Islamic State and al-Qaeda treat Westerners as walking leverage, and the collapse of French and American military footprints only made the market bigger.

Rideout’s release is one of the few recent wins in a region that keeps producing more captives than rescues.
