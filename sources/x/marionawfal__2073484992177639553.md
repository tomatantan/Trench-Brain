Geopolitical analyst Brandon Weichert breaks down the Russia-Ukraine stalemate everyone's arguing about, and his read cuts against most of what you're hearing.

Weichert's core argument: Putin isn't the hawk everyone thinks he is. He's the brake. Everyone around him in the Kremlin is telling him to hit harder, escalate, unleash what Russia's actually capable of. Putin's holding back because he still wants a deal with Trump. 

Weichert thinks that restraint has a shelf life, and Putin might not be able to hold that line much longer.

Here's the number that actually matters. Only 3 to 8% of Europe's combined military stockpile has gone to Ukraine since the war started, per the Kiel Institute. 

Russia's thrown its full military weight into this fight. Europe hasn't. That's the asymmetry driving the stalemate more than any battlefield heroics on either side.

Weichert says Zelensky's drone strikes into Russia aren't strength, but desperation. A play to unlock more U.S. aid while the front line is buckling.

And on Poland, he doesn't think Russia invades. But cyberattacks on NATO members, including Poland, he sees as increasingly likely. Not conquest. A signal that Moscow's patience with weapons and troops flowing through Polish territory into Ukraine is running out.

So is Putin really the last restraint on this war escalating, or is that giving him too much credit for a stalemate neither side can break?
