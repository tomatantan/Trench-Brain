🇪🇸🇲🇦 When smartphones replace tanks...how migration became a weapon in Ceuta

In a matter of hours in late July, 50-60 thousand people, overwhelmingly young men, crossed from Morocco into the Spanish enclave after online messages spread the promise of easy entry into Europe and the Schengen zone.

Spain’s reception system collapsed almost immediately.

The political shock, however, landed hard and fast.

This is the modern, accelerated version of what Belarus tried against Poland in 2021: using human movement, amplified by social media and economic desperation, as a deliberate tool to create crisis and test a state’s ability to control its own territory.

Poland treated the 2021 pressure as a hybrid attack on its sovereignty.

It refused mass entry, built physical barriers, conducted pushbacks, and ultimately denied Belarus the victory of an overwhelmed border.

Ceuta shows the same method can now be executed faster, cheaper, and with little more than smartphones and coordinated messaging.

The timing adds an extra layer of questions, Spain’s refusal to support U.S. operations against Iran, recent warm signals between Washington and Rabat, and Morocco’s long-standing claim on the enclave, but those remain circumstantial.

What is no longer theoretical is the tactic itself, migration turned into pressure, and sovereignty challenged without a single tank crossing the line.
