🇺🇸🇮🇷 The Iran war quietly exposed a scary hole in U.S. missile defenses.

The Army's 2027 budget asks for 3,655 interceptors, roughly 2,798 Patriot PAC-3 MSE and 857 THAAD. That's about 10 times what it bought in 2026.

The jump is massive next to recent years. THAAD buys were as low as 11 in 2024 and 12 in 2025. PAC-3 sat around 214 in 2025. The Army has also more than quadrupled its long-term PAC-3 target, from 3,376 missiles to 13,773.

Two things are driving it. The war with Iran drained interceptor stockpiles faster than anyone planned for, and the Pentagon is bracing for a much bigger fight with China in the Pacific.

Here's the catch. Ordering thousands of missiles is easy on paper. U.S. factories currently max out around 620 PAC-3 and 96 THAAD a year, so hitting these numbers means building the industry almost from scratch, and Congress still has to pay for it.

If Iran drained the shelves this fast, a war with China is the real nightmare math.
