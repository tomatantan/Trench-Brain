Andy walks through the fight sparked by Venice's new equity-token split. 

Tulip King's argument: the terminal value of the token is zero regardless of the business, because token holders don't own the business, they own an ERC20. 

Erik Voorhees pushed back, arguing equity holders hold the majority of the tokens, so incentives are aligned, and Venice will keep buying and burning. Andy's not buying it. 

His logic: the best use of a company's revenue is whatever benefits shareholders most, not token holders. 

That was true before the raise, and it's true after. If equity holders hold both equity and tokens, they get upside twice. Token-only holders get upside never, at the company's discretion.
