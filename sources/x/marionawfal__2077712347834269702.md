🇺🇸🇮🇷 The White House keeps crying wolf at Iran, and it's walking into a credibility trap.

Every time Trump draws a line in the sand with Iran, he sets a trap for himself.

Follow through and the strikes get more destructive.

Blink and he teaches Tehran that his deadlines are negotiable.

The pattern is by now almost mechanical. 

A dramatic deadline, an expletive-filled warning about "Power Plant Day" or a whole civilization dying tonight, then a last-minute postponement dressed up as productive diplomacy.

In March, Trump gave Iran 48 hours to open the Strait of Hormuz or watch its power plants get obliterated, then postponed 12 hours out, citing good conversations. 

This week ran the same script in miniature, with Trump floating a 20% fee on cargo crossing the strait, scrapping it inside 24 hours, and back to threatening bridges by nightfall.

The problem isn't any single reversal, but what the sequence trains Iran to expect. 

Deterrence runs on the other side believing the threat is real, and each walked-back deadline chips away at that belief.

A deadline that keeps moving eventually stops meaning anything, and each threat that keeps the peace today quietly raises the price of being believed tomorrow.
