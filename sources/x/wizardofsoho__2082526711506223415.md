1 min left to go for FOMC Rate decision 

Here's my take: 

A hold is the baseline for today’s decision, but the market is on high alert for a hawkish surprise or a setup for a hike later this year.

Heading into FOMC announcement, the consensus is that the Federal Reserve will keep the federal funds rate target at 3.50-3.75%. However, contrast that with early-year expectations of rate cuts, the conversation has completely flipped toward "Hold or Hike."  

Key Dynamics Driving the Current View

Rate Decision Expectations: 
Futures markets are pricing in roughly a 60–65% chance of a hold and a 35–40% chance of a 25 bps rate hike. While a pause remains the most likely outcome, a quarter-point increase isn't completely off the table.  

The Dual Pressure (Disinflation vs. Oil Spikes): 
June CPI eased to 3.5%, giving the Fed breathing room to hold. However, recent geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have pushed crude back toward $100/barrel, threatening to reignite energy-driven inflation. Added pressures from new trade tariffs and supply constraints in tech infrastructure continue to create persistent upside inflation risks.  

A Divided Committee:
 Internal division within the FOMC is at a high. Nearly half of the policymakers supported at least one rate hike before year-end, making the September and December meetings hot spots for potential tightening.  

The "Warsh Fed" Shift: 
Under Chair Kevin Warsh, the Fed has signaled zero tolerance for persistent inflation. Crucially, Warsh has moved to scale back forward guidance, meaning markets should expect a lean, minimal official statement with fewer explicit breadcrumbs about future policy moves.
