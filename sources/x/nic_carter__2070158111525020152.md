think from the perspective of a junk bond investor. what required rate of return would you require to hold the riskiest part (STRC) of the teetering edifice that is MSTR? subordinated under other real debt? with single asset collateral? with negative convexity baked in (yields rise as the collateral sells off)?. given the total lack of protections for "lenders"? that is clearly distressed? 

in my opinion, below CCC (deep junk), but above default. at least 1000 bps spread over SOFR

eyeballing it that means roughly somewhere in the 15-20% range. can Saylor pay that? at the moment he doesn't seem willing. so the only way for STRC holders to get that type of return is for STRC to drift off par, such that the implied yield goes up. 

at 11.5% current nominal yield, STRC would have to sell off to $76.67 for a 15% implied yield, or all the way to $57.50 for a 20% implied yield. and that, my friends, is why it's currently trading in the 70s. 

as a non junk bond investor, I don't have a great intuitive sense for the rate of return I would demand to hold it. I would probably want a minimum of 15%. 

I believe that because the structure is unsustainable and requires the perpetual monetization of the common equity which is trading near par, it will continue to trade at a discount unless Strategy hikes the yield on STRC to the appropriate range, which is 15-20% in my opinion.
