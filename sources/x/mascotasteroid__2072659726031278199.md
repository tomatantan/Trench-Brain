When We Look Up at the Stars

Throughout human history, every time we've looked up, the stars have been there.

Thousands of years ago, we navigated by the night sky, following the North Star to find our way home and weaving myths around the Milky Way. Today, we peer billions of light-years into the universe through powerful telescopes, launch rockets beyond Earth's atmosphere, and send probes to worlds that once existed only in our imagination.

Technology has transformed the way we explore the universe, but it has never changed the feeling we have when we look up at the stars.

Space is the farthest place we can reach, yet somehow the closest place to our dreams.

Out there, there are no borders, no languages, no wealth, no prejudice. From just a few hundred kilometers above Earth, every conflict seems insignificant, and every line we've drawn across the planet simply disappears. Astronauts often say that the moment they truly see Earth from space, they realize that humanity has always shared a single home.

Perhaps that's what makes space so extraordinary.
