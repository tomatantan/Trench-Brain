🇰🇼🇮🇷 Everyone's watching Qatar and the UAE, but Kuwait is the one sitting in the crosshairs.

Before this war it hosted roughly 13,000 American troops, more than any other GCC country, across Camp Arifjan, Ali Al Salem and Camp Buehringg It's also been the most relaxed about letting the U.S. run operations off its soil.

Qatar and the UAE hedge. They keep a channel open to Tehran and make themselves useful to both sides. Kuwait doesn't play that game, and unlike Bahrain it's big enough to actually matter. Kharg Island, the terminal handling most of Iran's crude exports, sits about 150 miles away.

Now the part that should terrify Kuwait City.

Kuwait has no rivers, no lakes, essentially zero natural fresh water. Around 90% of its drinking water comes from desalination plants bolted onto the same power stations that keep the country running. Hit the power, you lose the water. Lose the water in a country that clears 50°C in summer and you get a humanitarian collapse in days.

No cruise missiles needed. Short-range ballistic missiles and drones do the whole job, and take the American bases offline as a bonus.

There's history here. In 1987 Iran put a Silkworm missile into the Kuwaiti tanker Sea Isle City in Kuwaiti waters. Kuwait responded by reflagging its fleet under the American flag and inviting the U.S. Navy in to escort it. Tehran remembers exactly how that went.

Rough tiers of how the Gulf is lining up:

🇴🇲 Oman: friendly to Tehran, the permanent back channel

🇶🇦🇦🇪🇸🇦 Qatar, UAE, Saudi (maybe): limited cooperation, quietly restraining Washington

🇰🇼🇧🇭 Kuwait and Bahrain: fully in the U.S. and Israeli camp

🇮🇶🇯🇴 Iraq is its own mess, and Jordan belongs in the hostile column if you stretch the definition

Kuwait sits on 101 billion barrels of reserves and almost none of it buys protection here.

The states with the least leverage over Tehran are the ones with the most to lose.
