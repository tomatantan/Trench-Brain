🇪🇺🇺🇸 Could the EU finally step up with its own independent defense strategy?

America’s allies were built to rely on U.S. naval power, not replace it.

For decades Washington encouraged countries across Europe and East Asia to prioritize trade and economic growth while the U.S. patrolled the global oceans and handled security.

That system left most nations with limited long range naval forces, ships often sit in port, crews lack rapid deployment training, and power projection remains minimal except for a few like the UK, France, and Japan.

The current Iran conflict highlights the problem.

Allies weren’t looped in early, intelligence wasn’t shared, and now the U.S. expects support that simply isn’t ready on short notice.

This could be the push the EU needs for a real standalone defense approach, joint procurement, stronger naval capabilities, and reduced dependence on the American umbrella.

In the Pacific, South Korea, Japan, and especially Taiwan still lean heavily on U.S. forces for deterrence against North Korea and rising tensions with China.

These countries need stronger options if they want to maintain stability amid Beijing’s growing military assertiveness in the region.

The old post WWII bargain is straining.

Allies may soon face a choice: keep depending on Washington or start investing seriously in the hard power they’ve long outsourced.

The world is changing fast.
