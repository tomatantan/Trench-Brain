🇹🇼 Taiwan has the plan, the terrain, and weapons on order, but it lacks a legislature willing to make survival against China real.

On paper, asymmetric defense is strong with anti-ship missiles, HIMARS, mines, dispersed urban resistance, and civilian resilience training. 

In practice, parliament slashed the defense budget from $40B to $24.8B, wiped out domestic drone buys for this year, and axed $2B in U.S. joint drone R&D.

Shortages are stacking up: aging munitions, reserves that take up to 90 days to mobilize by paper notices, not enough rifles for a quarter of reservists, and failed logistics during exercises because commanders didn’t know troop locations. 

Taiwan’s greatest threat may not be China across the water, but its own politicians.

Source: Japan Times, Taiwan News / Writer: Monica
