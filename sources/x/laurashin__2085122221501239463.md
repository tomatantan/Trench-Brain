EtherFi CEO Mike Silagadze says frontends have an ethical duty to stop users from catastrophic trades

"We had the exact same situation where a couple of trades went through, not massive sums, but users lost a bunch of money because they were allowed to do something that reasonably we should not have allowed them to do. If somebody goes to Uniswap, picks some low liquidity token, this happened not long ago with Aave, someone went to the UI and said I want to sell 50 million ETH for 500 bucks, it's just madness"

"On one hand you could say, sorry bro, this is a permissionless smart contract, you pressed the button, you saw it in the UI. But if you're hosting the UI, you're playing some role there. Ethically, and I do mean ethically, you have a responsibility to prevent the user from doing something wildly insane. That's how brokerages operate, not that we're a brokerage, but if I go to Interactive Brokers and try to do some insane thing, it plays a role to stop me"

"I think Uniswap should do this, we're planning to do it as we roll some of this stuff out, and TradeXYZ should have done that. This isn't rocket science. If somebody tries to do a trade with 70% price impact or crazy slippage, you probably just shouldn't let them do that, forget warnings and red text, you just shouldn't let them do that"
