"Voter apathy is one of the classic DAO problems. The average token holder doesn't really want to spend their time governing a protocol"

Nick Almond, Head of Governance at Jito Foundation, on why DAO voter turnout is so broken and how to actually fix it

"There's limited reason, the average token holder doesn't really want to spend their time governing a protocol. So it tends to coalesce around a cohort of DAO-interested people, a cluster of professional delegates over in the EVM world that coalesced around almost every DAO. You get this shared culture that emerges mainly because there isn't broad participation from the wider token community"

"It leads to slow capture around the very most interested people, the ones willing to spend hours on governance forums. The biggest example the other way was Jupiter DAO on Solana, JupDAO, which was very retail heavy and consequently even more chaotic. The degens all turn up, stake their tokens for voting power, and it was pure chaos. High turnout wasn't great either"

"Until you get into things like decision markets, which Prophet will talk about, the best case is a highly curated set of delegates. At Jito DAO we have a curated set of delegates who represent the wider Solana ecosystem. They're not professional delegates trying to get paid or draw down the treasury, they're Solana aligned people who help us make good decisions. That curation is one of the best ways to do it"
