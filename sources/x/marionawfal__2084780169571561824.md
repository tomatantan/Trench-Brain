Current warfare ways allow you to do anything, but lecturing others on what they choose.

You can’t call mass migration a gift one day and a bioweapon the next.

The same voices that spent years insisting large scale migration is enrichment and moral progress are now treating it as a deliberate attack, but only because they believe the United States or Israel encouraged Morocco to push people into Spanish territory.

That switch exposes the game.

In modern hybrid warfare, almost anything can be turned into a weapon. For example: energy, information, cyber operations, or the movement of people.

Once demographic pressure becomes one more available tool, selective moral lectures stop working.

Look at the record. In late July this year, tens of thousands, estimates ran from 50,000 to nearly 70,000, surged into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta in a matter of days.

Spanish officials and local leaders openly accused Morocco of relaxing controls or actively facilitating the crossings, just as Rabat did in 2021 when more than 10,000 people poured across during a diplomatic spat.

The same method has been used by Belarus against Poland and Lithuania in 2021, and by Turkey against Greece whenever Ankara wanted leverage over the EU.

In each case the flow of people was treated as an instrument of statecraft.

Yet the language flips depending on who is blamed.

When migration serves progressive narratives it is humanitarian progress, but when the same movement is attributed to a rival it suddenly becomes hostile pressure designed to punish and destabilize.

Hybrid conflict does not care about consistency, you can choose the weapon that fits the moment.

You just cannot pretend the principle only applies when your opponents use it.
