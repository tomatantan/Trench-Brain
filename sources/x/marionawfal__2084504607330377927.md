🚨🇺🇸 🇮🇷 American crude exports fell to an eight-month low in July as the temporary peace let Middle Eastern oil back onto the market.

-U.S. crude exports dropped to 3.66 million barrels a day, the weakest since November, per Reuters

-The Asian share collapsed from 52% in June to 40% in July, with shipments to Japan down 67% and to South Korea down 39%

-Buyers went back to Gulf barrels once the temporary U.S.-Iran arrangement eased flows, choosing shorter voyages over American crude

-Domestic refineries ran at 96.3% capacity, soaking up oil that would otherwise have sailed

-Analysts expect a rebound above 4 million bpd across August and September, helped by wider WTI discounts and tanker bookings already picking up

For months this war made American oil the safe option, and Asian refiners paid the premium for barrels that did not have to pass Iran. 

One partial pause and they went straight back, which tells you the loyalty was never to the supplier, only to the risk.
