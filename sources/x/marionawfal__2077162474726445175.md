🇺🇸 Lindsey Graham was the archetype of the establishment hawk who learned how to survive and even thrive inside a MAGA admin.

He publicly warned that Trump could destroy democratic institutions and broke with him after January 6, calling it a grave threat to the rule of law and peaceful power transfer.

Then he reversed course once Trump regained power, golfing with him and becoming one of his most loyal Senate voices.

That gap between what Graham said and what he ultimately did may define his legacy more than his policy wins.

He was a consistent hardliner on foreign policy, strong support for Ukraine, Israel, confronting Iran and China, and believed access to power let him shape outcomes.

Figures like Marco Rubio and Pete Hegseth represent the next generation of hawks who’ve also made their peace with Trump’s style.

But Graham was the original bridge: the guy who could deliver Senate votes and hawkish credibility while bending to the political reality of the moment.

His death leaves a hole in that wing of the party and raises questions about whether future hawks will have the same willingness (or ability) to navigate the tensions between principle and power.
