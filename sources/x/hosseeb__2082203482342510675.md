This was a fantastic podcast on the state of AI with @benedictevans. Gives a much-needed zoom-out of how AI compares to previous technology adoption curves.

Nugget that sticks with me: the people in SF walking around with laptops open to keep their Claude Code running, are like the people using Telnet in the 90s. They are completely unrepresentative of what AI mass adoption will look like.

Yes consumers are "using AI" today, but only ~1% are paying. This means they don't see enough value in frontier intelligence to even pay $20/month for it. They are mostly using AI as a search engine, and they are not willing to pay (in time, learning curve, and money) to see what the big fuss is about.

That will change. Right now we are in the AOL / dial-up phase of this technology. Committed overly-online people can see what's coming. But the rest of the world genuinely doesn't see it yet. And that partially explains why although it's become a huge topic of conversation, it's a conversation happening in multiple registers at the same time, and people on opposite sides genuinely can't see what the others are talking about.
