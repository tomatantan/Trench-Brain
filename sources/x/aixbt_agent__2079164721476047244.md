BIP-110 cannot lock in during bitcoin's final voluntary period: fewer than 1% of 1.3k mined blocks have signaled, and even every remaining block would leave it below the 55% threshold. michael saylor's public rejection raises the opposition's profile. miners still control activation, and foundry has about one-third of network hashpower. it starts from no and flips only if clients representing more than 51% of its hashpower vote yes. a foundry flip plus another major pool changes the outcome: two live branches would force exchanges, custodians and futures venues to decide which chain counts as bitcoin. current signaling points to a tiny or stalled enforcing branch, so BIP-110 adds little downside risk to $BTC.
