🇮🇷 🇺🇸 Oil is doing something weird right now.

The Strait of Hormuz is basically shut, a deal keeps slipping away, and yet Brent is sitting near $88, still miles below last month's $100+ and May's $110 peak.

The reason is that traders are betting on a fudge. Some kind of messy half-deal that lets a bit of oil flow through the strait again. 

So they're pricing in the happy ending, not the reality on the water. And the reality is grim. 

Just 6 ships crossed Hormuz yesterday (pre-war it was 130 to 140).

What's kept prices this calm is a stack of cushions. 

Alternative export routes, softer demand, extra production, and above all China. Beijing singlehandedly balanced the market in May by slashing its imports. 

But those imports are climbing back through July and August, and crude can't stay down forever.

Meanwhile Trump is playing it cool, telling Axios he's "low-keying it" and leaning on economic pressure rather than fresh strikes. Tehran isn't blinking.

If the standoff drags into Q4 and inventories keep draining, analysts see a tipping point that could send oil to $120 to $140.

For now the market's pricing in optimism, but optimism has a poor track record in that strait.
