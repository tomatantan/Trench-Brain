Consortium stablecoins like OpenUSD are great because they put pressure on today's incumbents, but I don't think they automatically take market share from USDT or USDC. Circle/Tethers biggest moat isn't tech, it's liquidity, integrations, and global distribution.

Getting millions of users, exchanges, wallets, and merchants to switch is incredibly difficult. Where I do see consortium stablecoins winning is enterprise treasury, cross company settlement, and behind the scenes payment rails where the end user doesn't even know which stablecoin is being used.

The bigger impact may not be usage anymore but economics. Reserve revenue gets shared across a consortium instead of accruing to a single issuer, stablecoin issuers will need to find new ways to monetize beyond simply holding the float.

Tether has already started diversifying from stablecoins into AI, mining, and startup investments. Circle will likely have to deepen its distribution, expand its product suite, or pursue M&A to defend its position. The stablecoin war is moving from issuance to distribution.
