🇨🇳🇭🇰🇺🇸 Hong Kong is quietly beating U.S. chip controls

Washington spent years building walls to keep advanced chips out of China, and one small city is walking them straight through the front gate.

Over the first 5 months of 2026 China pulled in a record $239 billion in chip imports, and more than half of that flowed through Hong Kong. A decade ago the city handled about a third.

The setup is almost too easy. Hong Kong is a free port with no tariffs, money that moves without friction, and one of the best air cargo hubs on the planet, so high-value chips land and move on fast, few questions asked.

That leaves one small city sitting as the single most important gateway in China's scramble for the exact chips the U.S. keeps trying to fence off.

Turns out the fastest way past a wall is a good airport.
