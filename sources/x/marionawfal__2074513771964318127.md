🇪🇺🇺🇸 Europe wants to defend itself without America, but doesn't actually believe it can.

A new Public First poll of 24 EU countries found most think it's fine to lean on Europe for military help, and very few would accept help from outside, meaning the U.S.

But when asked whether their country is actually ready to defend itself, the answers get shy. A clear majority say no.

Then there's Finland, quietly ruining the average. It's the only country where most people think their country is ready to fight alone.

The difference isn't budget lines or slogans. Finland has a real conscription program, a trained reserve, and has spent decades preparing specifically for a Russian attack. 

The rest of Europe is trying to buy readiness like it's next-day delivery.

That's the gap the Ankara summit keeps dancing around. Rutte can call for a defense industrial revolution all he wants, but factories don't produce trained citizens, cohesive doctrine, or a public that trusts its own military.

Europe's problem isn't just the spending, it's that credible defense takes decades to build, and most of the continent forgot to start.
