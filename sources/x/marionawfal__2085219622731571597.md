🇮🇷 Big Oil dropped one of its strongest quarters on record.

Nearly $44 billion in combined profit.

ExxonMobil: $14.7 billion
Chevron: $12.1 billion (an all-time company record) 
Shell: $9.8 billion

The Iran conflict sent crude prices climbing hard: Higher oil, fatter refining margins. 

The majors took every bit of it.

The only quarter that ever beat this was the wild 2022 energy shock after Russia invaded Ukraine.

Because the Strait of Hormuz traffic collapsed down as much as 97% from the normal ~20 million barrels a day, that removed roughly 17% of global oil supply, the largest single disruption ever recorded.

Brent crude surged past $100 and briefly touched $126.

When a chokepoint this critical gets threatened, the risk premium hits fast, and the profits follow even faster.

What does a disruption of this scale tell you?
