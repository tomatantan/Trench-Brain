🇺🇸 🇮🇱 🇵🇸 Publicly, Netanyahu rejected Trump's Gaza disarmament plan. Privately, he's already implementing it.

Jared Kushner is heading to Israel next week, his first trip there since January, for talks on the next phase of the Gaza plan: disarming Hamas and a potential Israeli withdrawal. 

He's bringing Nickolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace's "high representative," and may hit other regional capitals too.

Under pressure from his coalition and with an October election looming, Netanyahu publicly rejected the disarmament plan. Days earlier, on a call with Kushner, he'd promised to give it a chance and pledged to pull back strikes on Gaza.

Washington's read on this is unbothered, almost knowing: officials are calling Netanyahu's rejection "election-season politics" rather than an actual policy shift, and say they're fine with the theater as long as he keeps doing what's asked on the ground.

One official says it quite clearly: 

"We understand Bibi's political needs. We have no problem with it as long as he continues to do what we ask."
