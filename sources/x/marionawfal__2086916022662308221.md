🇸🇦 🇹🇷 🇵🇰 🇪🇬 Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed the "Mecca" mutual defense pact last week. 

Egypt, despite months of quiet 4-way talks between all 4 countries, wasn't at the table when it was signed.

The reported reasons: Egypt doesn't want to be forced to pick a side between this bloc and its parallel relationships with Israel, the UAE and India. 

Cairo also has constitutional limits on deploying troops abroad, doesn't want to inherit Turkey's disputes with Greece and Cyprus or Pakistan's standoff with India, and would rather lean on its own leverage, the Suez Canal, than sign binding commitments that drag it into Gulf conflicts it didn't choose. 

The claim making the rounds online: a single unverified report (circulating via a pro-Kremlin aggregator, not picked up by any mainstream outlet) alleges Egypt was fed "bait" intelligence by the other three to test its loyalty, then caught passing it to the U.S. 

after which it was deemed untrustworthy and cut out.

Treat that second version as an unverified claim, not a confirmed account. Crazy story though if true!
