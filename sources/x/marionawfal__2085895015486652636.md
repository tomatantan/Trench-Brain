How close is NATO to real tech autonomy that doesn’t need the U.S. as a backstop?

The alliance is boosting funding for AI, autonomy, quantum and biotech startups, while the UK links 35 universities to turn defence research into usable capabilities.

Europe already leads in scientific publications across several of these areas, yet it continues to lag badly when it comes to patents, venture capital and the ability to scale production into actual systems that troops can use.

Critical digital infrastructure, high-end platforms and key software still lean heavily on American suppliers, which means genuine independence remains distant.

Ukraine has shown how quickly battlefield pressure can force rapid iteration from lab to frontline, but Western Europe is trying to build that same ecosystem without the same urgency.

These programmes are closing some of the gap and creating better pathways for dual-use innovation.

Standing fully alone without U.S. technology, capital and industrial depth is still years away.
