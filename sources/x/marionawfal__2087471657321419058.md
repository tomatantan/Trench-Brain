🇬🇧 Britain has spent the last decade quietly turning its streets into a fortress against vehicle attacks.

Bollards, barriers and gates are now disguised as flower pots, benches, sculptures and even the giant Arsenal letters outside the Emirates. 

The industry has grown into a multi-billion-pound business. Britain has planted more bollards than trees in the last 10 years.

Next year, Martyn’s Law will require venues holding more than 200 people to prepare for terrorist attacks.

But at some point the conversation has to go beyond building stronger roadblocks.

Stopping dangerous people from reaching the point where they want to mow down crowds seems a lot more important than endlessly redesigning cities around the possibility that they will.
