I sat down with PhD economist Chris Martenson on the oil market numbers that genuinely don't add up.

39 ships through Hormuz in 24 hours, half what the Trump administration is publicly claiming, a 3rd of pre-war traffic. Yet crude sits near pre-war lows. 

Meanwhile money managers are the most bearish on oil they've been in 15 years (not slightly bearish, the most bearish on record) at the exact moment physical supply is the tightest it's been all year.

Martenson's 2 explanations: either some of the smartest people in finance have simply lost the thread, or someone with deep pockets is running a standing sell order timed to peace deal headlines, letting momentum algorithms pile on and crater the price on cue regardless of what's happening at the strait.

The part that should worry people more than the price: U.S. oil product demand is up 2.9% year over year while the Strategic Petroleum Reserve and commercial stockpiles sit at multi-decade lows for this time of year.

The concern isn't that oil is expensive right now. It's that if the ceasefire cracks again, America hits a real shortage with nothing left in the reserve to cushion it (having spent the intervening weeks burning through what remained to maintain the illusion of cheap oil.)

Is this algorithmic noise or engineered price suppression while the physical reserves quietly run dry?
