🇺🇸🇪🇺 The EU has just approved a temporary bill that gives permission to social media platforms to check on private messages under the guise of clamping down on child abuse.

It comes alongside expanding hate speech laws, the Digital Services Act, AI-powered content moderation, and growing legal pressure against anonymous online communication across Europe. 

Each piece of legislation is presented as a small intervention to address a pressing problem.

Together, they move in the same direction: less anonymity, less privacy, more monitoring, and greater regulation of how people communicate.

That shift is happening at the same time people are becoming increasingly accustomed to living without meaningful privacy. 

Years of social media, smartphones, data collection, and AI have normalized constant surveillance, making each new expansion easier to accept than the last.

The result is a gradual convergence of policies and technologies that steadily reduce civil liberties until even private communication isn't private anymore. 

In each case, they claim the reforms are to protect vulnerable people and clamp down on hate speech, and that has brought us to an unprecedented place where we're only a stone's throw away from the end of online anonymity.
