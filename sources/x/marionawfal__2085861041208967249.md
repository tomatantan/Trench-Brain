🇺🇸🇨🇳 The U.S. builds its weapons with Chinese parts, Trump wants that gone

The administration just dropped over $2 billion on battery and critical-mineral companies, part of a bigger campaign to yank the U.S. off Chinese suppliers.

Where it landed:

$1.4 billion Pentagon loan to battery maker Sila Nanotechnologies

$400 million to Sunrise Energy Metals for scandium, which toughens aircraft aluminum

$150 million to Niron Magnetics for rare-earth-free magnets used in missiles and phones

$85.5 million stake in Strategic Bauxite, the raw material behind aluminum

The reason is cold national security. Batteries and metals power drones, AI data centers and weapons, and America still runs to China for a chunk of it. 

Trump says they've done 160 minerals deals worth nearly $40 billion.

The catch: on some of these Washington is taking equity stakes, like it did with Intel, which is a long way from how the U.S. usually treats private business. 

And they're slow bets, years to build, and mining ventures often die on cost or busted tech.
