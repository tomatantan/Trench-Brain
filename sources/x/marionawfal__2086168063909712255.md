🇺🇸 The U.S. is unintentionally pushing allies toward defense autonomy and the new Saudi Turkey Pakistan pact is the latest proof.

Signed in Mecca with a mutual “attack on one is attack on all” clause, this trilateral deal shows major regional powers no longer want to rely solely on the American security umbrella, especially amid the Iran conflict.

It’s the same story we saw in Europe: Trump’s tough talk on NATO “freeloading,” threats of troop cuts, and reduced commitments forced Europeans to boost their own spending and talk openly about strategic autonomy.

The pattern repeats across regions, from the Gulf hedging bets to Asia watching U.S.-China tensions, as countries build overlapping alliances for independence when Washington’s approach feels unpredictable.

The old post WWII reliance model is cracking.

What we’re getting isn’t a clean multipolar world, but a more fractured one where regions pursue their own narrow needs, rivalries, and interests, often with less coordination and more instability.
