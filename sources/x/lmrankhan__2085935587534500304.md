On average every new tech wave seems to last around 25 to 30 years.

Each wave is split into three phases. It starts with infrastructure and experimentation, then moves into the PMF and adoption phase, and eventually ends with mature incumbents.

The early investors can make the best multiples, the middle is where the most money gets made, and the late stage is usually the safest money.

Crypto’s timeline started in 2009. My view is that in 2026 crypto is just moving from the early phase into the middle.

The infrastructure is finally starting to work, distribution is getting much larger, but the eventual apps and winners still haven’t really been crowned yet.

I don’t believe we’re entering the boring maturity phase at all. If anything, crypto might just be hitting its stride.

I also think a lot of VCs that left crypto will eventually come back in some shape. But I don’t really fault them for leaving. It’s healthy for crypto focused funds to branch into AI, robotics, fintech, energy and other frontier tech.

Long term that might be better for crypto anyway. Crypto shouldn’t live in its own bubble forever. It should slowly become part of the broader frontier tech stack.

There could be huge crossover benefits when the biggest investors in AI and robotics are also crypto investors, and when the best companies in those categories naturally start using crypto rails where they make sense.

The next question is whether crypto has already produced most of its enduring app categories and or dominant incumbents. I don’t think it has. The show must go on.
