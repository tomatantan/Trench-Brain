🇰🇷 South Korea’s sudden semiconductor crash last month, where leveraged bets on Samsung and SK Hynix triggered cascading margin calls, may be a preview of where investing across the developed world is heading.

Many investors correctly identified Samsung and SK Hynix as some of the biggest winners of the AI boom; the problem wasn't their investment thesis.

It was that many used so much leverage that an ordinary market correction triggered margin calls and forced selling before their position had time to recover.

The more important question is why so many people are feeling increasingly compelled to take the risks that cause such crashes. 

When the traditional middle-class path of working, saving, and buying a home no longer feels realistic, speculation starts looking less like recklessness and more like necessity.

The U.S is moving in the same direction.

A handful of companies like NVIDIA, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, and Meta now account for an extraordinary share of the market, while millions of ordinary people are becoming more active investors than ever before.

That combination will likely change how public companies behave.

The competition won't just be to build the best products; it will increasingly be to tell the best story.

The companies that dominate attention, social media, and investor sentiment may command valuations that drift further and further from their underlying fundamentals.

That could accelerate one of the biggest wealth transfers in modern times. 

When ordinary people turn to speculation due to increasing inequalities, the wealthy will be better positioned to recognize when the story has outrun reality and exit before the narrative turns.

Everyone else will perpetually buy into yesterday's narrative while the smart money is already chasing the next one.
