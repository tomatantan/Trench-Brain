🇮🇷🇺🇸 Both exits are blocked now

Saudi Arabia built its escape from Iranian leverage on one assumption: if Hormuz closes, pump the oil west across the peninsula and ship it out of Yanbu on the Red Sea. 

That plan worked. It carried millions of barrels a day while the strait strangled.

Then the Houthis started hitting Saudi tankers near Bab el-Mandeb and declaring the waterway theirs to authorize.

That changes the arithmetic completely. 

One threatened chokepoint is a regional problem with a workaround. 

Two threatened chokepoints on opposite coasts leaves the world's biggest exporter with no clean way out at all, and the bypass infrastructure the Gulf spent billions on becomes a pipeline to a second blockade.

The market read it instantly. 

Brent jumped nearly six dollars in a single session and crossed $100 for the first time since May, while American gas sits above $4.

Iran never needed to close the Middle East. 

It needed both of its doors watched, and now they are.
