The first signs of "consciousness" in AI?

Anthropic built a new tool to peek inside modern LLMs to see what they're "thinking."

Here's how it works (and what this new research from Anthropic means in plain English):

When a language model "thinks," it's doing a ton of internal math across thousands of neurons - most of that is just automatic, mechanical processing (like your brain digesting food or regulating your heartbeat - you have no access to it and don't need to).

The new tool from Anthropic is called a "Jacobian lens," which is essentially a way to peek inside the model while it's generating a response and ask:

"Of everything happening in there right now, which parts actually matter for what you're about to say next?"

What they found in the "workspace":

Reportable: LLMs can describe them if asked ("what are you thinking about?")

Steerable: Researchers can reach in and swap one concept for another, and the model's answer changes accordingly.

Simple analogy: Imagine your brain has a "mental notepad" where you jot down the 3-4 things you're actively juggling in conscious thought.

This paper finds LLMs seem to have something like that notepad too: a small, privileged workspace of "active thoughts" floating around.

Anthropic doesn't claim LLMs are conscious, but this is interesting to think about...
