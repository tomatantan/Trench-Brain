🇮🇶🇸🇾 The Kirkuk–Baniyas pipeline, shut since the 1980s, is being brought back into focus as a way to move Iraqi crude to the Mediterranean instead of relying heavily on the Hormuz route. 

The full rehabilitation could take 2–3 years and cost $4.5–8B, but the project will have an initial transport capacity of up to 2 million barrels per day of crude oil.

They will pass through Syria, with Western oil firms playing a role to handle much of the technical and financing work.

Baghdad wants a more secure, westward-facing energy path, and this pipeline is being treated as a strategic reset, not just an infrastructure repair.
