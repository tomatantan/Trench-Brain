🇪🇸 Spain's prime minister got booed and cursed to his face when he landed in Ceuta.

Pedro Sánchez was jeered by furious crowds on arrival Friday, a day after roughly 60,000 people crossed from Morocco into a city of about 83,000, with 57 dead in the water and the crush at the fence.

Resident Rocío Gil broke down on camera describing how people there feel: 

"We feel totally abandoned, the outlook is catastrophic... we cannot call the police because they are overrun"

Locals held signs reading "These clowns have sold us out," and one man told Antena 3 that Sánchez "is to blame for all of this".

Vox leader Santiago Abascal called him a "traitor and mafioso who behaves like an enemy of Spain," while Madrid's regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso accused him of letting the crossings happen to bury the corruption cases circling his party.

Around 48,000 had already gone back to Morocco by Friday afternoon, most realizing they had little chance of staying legally.

The fury at Sánchez is real and the response was his to run, but the fence people are blaming him for has two sides, and Rabat has spent years loosening its grip on that border whenever it wants to make a point about Western Sahara or about who it thinks these two cities belong to.
