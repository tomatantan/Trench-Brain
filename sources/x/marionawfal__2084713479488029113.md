🇺🇸 Apple did something last night you probably didn't notice.

For about 40 minutes, Telegram just vanished from the App Store, cutting off more than 1 billion users. Apple then quietly put it back. Its review had found child abuse material posted by a user.

Founder Pavel Durov tells a bigger story. He says it was a targeted hit by a "takedown extortionist," someone who plants illegal content in a public group, hides it by editing an old message so members can't see or report it, then reports it straight to Apple.

The goal is ransom: these crews shake down group owners, and the ones who refuse to pay get their community, or the whole app, flagged for removal.

What's rattling developers is Apple's response. It nuked an app used by over 1 billion people before it even contacted Telegram. Epic's Tim Sweeney jumped in, asking how any messaging app survives that standard, iMessage included.

Nobody's arguing illegal content should stay up. The unsettling part is how easily one bad actor can trick a gatekeeper into pulling the trigger on everyone.
