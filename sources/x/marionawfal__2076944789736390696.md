🇾🇪🇮🇷🇸🇦 The Houthis control the strait that could choke the global economy, and it isn't Hormuz. 

With Iran already strangling that one, its Yemeni allies barely have to fire to squeeze the next.

At the southern end of the Red Sea sits the Bab el-Mandeb, a passage under 20 miles wide. 

Around 12% of global trade runs through this corridor, worth more than $1 trillion a year.

The vulnerability traces back to Hormuz. With that strait choked, Saudi Arabia rerouted its oil overland to Red Sea ports and out through the Bab el-Mandeb, flows that nearly doubled to 7.2 million barrels a day in April. 

The strait everyone overlooked became the release valve for the one everyone watches.

Once the risk is there, carriers reroute around Africa, premiums spike, and weeks get added to every voyage.

The counterweight here is real. Unlike Hormuz, the Bab el-Mandeb can't be sealed, since ships can still exit north through Suez. 

Most analysts doubt the Houthis will try, since a closure would batter China, Egypt, and Saudi Arabia too.

So it's leverage, not a doomsday switch. But in a war already strangling one chokepoint, a second one held hostage may be all Iran needs.
