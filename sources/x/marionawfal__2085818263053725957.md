🇨🇩 DRC's Ebola outbreak is now the second-largest ever 

This outbreak just crossed 4,000 cases, and it's already the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record. 1,801 deaths.

For comparison: at the same 11-week mark, this outbreak has 8 times more cases and 6 times more deaths than the 2014-16 West Africa epidemic. The one that eventually killed over 11,000 people.

Officials are now checking something worse than the case count: whether the virus itself is mutating, because the severity here is "unprecedented" for this particular strain.

2/3s of deaths are happening in the community, not in treatment centers. At one MSF facility, 90% of patients weren't even on the official contact list.
That's the real problem hiding under the numbers: contact tracers are finding 10 contacts per patient. The expected number is 40. Most of the spread isn't being tracked at all.

Africa CDC's response: stop waiting for cases to come in, start going door to door instead.

There's a narrow bright spot. Early data suggests a vaccine built for a different Ebola strain caused zero deaths among people who'd received it, so they're testing whether it works here too. 

But the outbreak's own side effects are stacking up; child measles vaccinations down 69% in the hardest-hit town, as fear keeps families away from clinics entirely.

Contact tracing was supposed to contain this. It's undercounting by 4x. What does "door to door" actually catch that months of standard response already missed?
