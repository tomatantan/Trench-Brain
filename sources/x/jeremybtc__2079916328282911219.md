A crypto wallet app LEAKED people's private keys for SEVEN years, and nobody noticed until last week.

The app was Zilliqa's Ledger app, live since 2019.

Every signature is supposed to be protected by a random number generated fresh each time.

Zilliqa's app copied the wrong bytes, and left part of that random number fixed at zero.

That tiny predictable piece was enough. Every transaction you signed gave away a little more of your key.

After about five transactions, an attacker can rebuild the entire key in seconds on a normal laptop.

This sat undetected in every version of the app for seven years.

It was only caught last week when someone started actively draining wallets.

The worst part is it can't be reversed. Those weak signatures are on the blockchain forever.

Affected users can't even move their funds to safety. The attacker already has the key and can beat them to it.

Seven years of a hardware wallet app quietly wrote everyone's private keys into public view.
