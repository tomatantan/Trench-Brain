🇺🇸🇷🇺 U.S. intelligence has changed its assessment on Putin.

For years the working assumption was that Russia was too bogged down in Ukraine to risk a direct confrontation with NATO. 

That view is now being revised.

New reports detailed by U.S. officials warn that Putin could test the alliance with a limited or hybrid operation, potentially as soon as this fall, and out to 2029.

The scenarios on the table are deliberately designed to stay below the threshold of a clear Article 5 trigger:

- Major cyberattacks  
- Sabotage of critical infrastructure  
- Unmarked or deniable forces  
- A small-scale land probe along the eastern flank (Baltics or Poland most often mentioned)

The goal would be to force NATO into the gray zone; where attribution is murky, the response has to be proportional, and every one of the 32 members has to decide how hard to push back.

What’s driving the shift? Mounting pressure inside Russia, Ukrainian strikes deeper into Russian territory, and slower-than-expected battlefield gains. 

Officials now assess that limited escalation could start looking more attractive to the Kremlin, not less.

The real test is political will: Can the alliance stay unified when Moscow deliberately makes the line between “aggression” and “war” hard to define?

This is the kind of pressure point that could reshape European security for years.
