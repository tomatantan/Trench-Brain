🇺🇸🇮🇷 Containment worked against the Soviets, it mostly held in America’s own hemisphere. Against Iran it keeps producing the opposite result.

For decades Washington treated containment as the safe default.

George Kennan’s strategy squeezed the USSR without direct war until the system cracked from within.

In Latin America the same logic of isolation, pressure, and proxies was applied to socialist and communist governments, accepting imperfect outcomes rather than risking full-scale invasion.

The approach felt familiar and manageable, but Iran has broken that pattern.

Years of sanctions and isolation were already hardening the regime.

The 2026 conflict then handed Tehran something it never fully possessed before: demonstrated ability to disrupt and partially control the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway that still carries a large share of global oil.

U.S. intelligence assessments now describe that capability as lasting.

At the same time the campaign burned through critical American stockpiles, Patriots, THAAD interceptors, Tomahawks, at rates that will take years to rebuild, leaving less margin for higher-priority theaters.

The result is not a weaker, more isolated Iran.

It is a regime that has turned a theoretical geographic advantage into practical leverage, used the fighting to tighten internal control, and forced negotiations over the very chokepoint Washington once took for granted.

Containment delivered results elsewhere.

In the Persian Gulf it is delivering the strategic opposite of what it was designed to achieve.
