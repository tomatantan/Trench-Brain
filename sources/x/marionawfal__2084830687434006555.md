🚨🇺🇸 A Michigan voter told CNN exactly why she was voting the way she was: 

"I'm not voting for anybody who takes money from AIPAC. Because the money should be spent here, not over there."

Something has inverted. 

For four decades, AIPAC's endorsement functioned as a credential, proof a candidate was serious and safe. 

Now the check itself is the attack ad, and the group's own spending is the thing opponents run against.

New York proved the model last month. 

Brad Lander unseated Dan Goldman in a race that turned on Israel policy.

Candidates making that argument also won in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Colorado.

Tonight Michigan is testing whether it scales to a battleground Senate seat, and the early returns are ugly for the establishment. 

AIPAC's super PAC poured more than $30 million into boosting Haley Stevens in what became the most expensive Democratic Senate primary in American history. 

Abdul El-Sayed, who made that spending his central argument, leads in early counting. 

The voter's reasoning is the part that should worry the people writing the checks, because it has nothing to do with the Middle East.

It's a household weighing $30 million spent on one primary against what her own community is going without, in a year of $4 gas and a war two-thirds of Americans say wasn't worth it.

That's the shift, and no amount of super PAC money reverses it.
