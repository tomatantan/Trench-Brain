🇺🇸🇪🇸 Asmongold's Twitch ban raises a much bigger question than whether what he said was disgusting

Days after the mass migrant influx into Spain's Ceuta enclave, Twitch suspended Asmongold after he argued that authorities should shoot people attempting to cross the border illegally, even saying women and children shouldn't be exempt.

His comments were extreme. But the free-speech question is more complicated than simply deciding whether they're offensive: he wasn't instructing his audience to kill anyone; he was advocating a government policy involving lethal force.

A free-speech absolutist would argue that even grotesque policy proposals must remain lawful political speech. Other democracies draw the boundary much earlier, criminalizing some expression deemed to incite hatred or violence against protected groups.

And that's where this stops being about Asmongold.

If an idea is considered too extreme to advocate, somebody has to decide where "extreme" begins. Today that might mean shooting border crossers. Tomorrow it could mean mass deportations, abolishing prisons, revolutionary politics, or some policy nobody has even proposed yet.

Democracy isn't tested by whether people are allowed to advocate ideas everyone already finds acceptable.

It's tested by who gets to decide which unacceptable ideas you're forbidden from advocating.
