One narrow strip of water between Africa and the Arabian Peninsula handles 15% of all global seaborne trade. Most people couldn't find it on a map. 

The Houthi attacks in 2023 showed exactly how this works. Maersk, MSC, and every major carrier rerouted around Africa overnight. 6,500 extra kilometers, 10 extra days, costs that quietly found their way into prices on shelves in cities that had never heard of Bab el-Mandeb.

Nothing announced itself. Inflation just got a little stickier, shipping took a little longer, and the news moved on.

Now we have the Strait of Hormuz disrupted, Suez volumes collapsed, and a land bridge across Saudi Arabia carrying cargo in trucks. The global shipping system is simultaneously rerouting around 3 crises at once.

The last time supply chains ran this hot, everything got more expensive for 2 years. We're early in this one.
