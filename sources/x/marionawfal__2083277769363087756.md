🇮🇷🇺🇸 The Iran war isn't nearing an end. It's becoming a permanent, deliberately managed conflict.

Most people see two possible outcomes: Either Trump escalates, or he makes a deal with Iran.

Geopolitical analyst Patrick Henningsen thinks that's a false choice; he believes Washington is preparing to do both.

Fight, pause, negotiate, rearm, and fight again.

According to Patrick, the periods of diplomacy are not necessarily designed to produce peace; they're created to buy time.

He argues the Pentagon's long-term objective hasn't changed.

Continue degrading Iran's military infrastructure, try to weaken Tehran's ability to control the Strait of Hormuz, and keep applying pressure until its strategic position steadily erodes.

Whether that takes months or years.

That's why he doesn't expect a sudden breakthrough where Washington simply accepts Iran's demands and walks away.

He thinks we're entering a cycle, because this war is no longer just about Iran.

It's becoming a contest over the entire trade network linking Iran, Russia, China and India.

Hormuz is only one front.

The Caspian Sea, the north-south trade corridor, China's Belt and Road Initiative, strategic ports. and critical pipelines are all on the board.

In Patrick's view, Washington isn't simply trying to pressure Tehran; it's trying to prevent an alternative Eurasian economic system from emerging outside U.S influence.

That also explains why the battlefield keeps expanding.
