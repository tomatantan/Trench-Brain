🇺🇸🇮🇷 Trump may have reached the point where every option makes America's position worse.

Middle East expert Joshua Landis says Iran isn't just retaliating to U.S. pressure anymore. It's deliberately expanding the battlefield to convince Washington that escalation carries a higher cost than compromise.

Start with the recent strikes.

An American response inside Iran is followed by Iranian attacks on U.S. assets in Jordan and on ships in Egypt.

If Washington turns to economic warfare or tries to blockade Iran, Tehran will simply drag the conflict back into military escalation. Landis agrees.

He argues the White House still believes it can force Iran to back down by gradually degrading its economy, missile capabilities and military infrastructure.

But every new round of pressure pushes Iran to widen the conflict instead, creating economic pain for America's regional partners and demonstrating that no country is insulated from the consequences.

Egypt is the perfect example.

Even if American ships are the intended target, attacks near Egypt threaten tourism, Suez Canal traffic and one of the region's most fragile economies.

The point isn't necessarily Egypt itself.

It's proving that pressure on Iran can quickly become pressure on everyone else.

That brings Landis to the real issue: the Strait of Hormuz.

He argues this war is ultimately about who controls one of the world's most important energy chokepoints.

For decades, American dominance in the Gulf has underpinned its global influence.

But Landis says preserving that dominance through military force is becoming increasingly difficult.

His argument is that even if Washington steps back, Iran cannot simply shut off global oil forever.

Tehran also depends on exporting its own energy, meaning every Gulf state ultimately has an incentive to reach some form of accommodation.

The real question isn't whether oil will keep flowing, but whether the United States remains the power that guarantees it.

Landis warns that continuing to escalate may actually accelerate the decline Washington is trying to prevent.
