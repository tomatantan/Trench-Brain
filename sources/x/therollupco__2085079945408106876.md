The Daily Equity by @DinariGlobal

The AI choke point almost nobody's pricing in is the optical cables that connect the GPUs. And China controls two-thirds of them. 

@robbieklages on why this proposed ban reshapes the whole data-center buildout:

The setup (per Counterpoint Research): as AI shifts to agentic workloads and massive GPU superclusters, copper cabling hits its physical limit, the "copper wall", forcing a move to high-speed optical (400G, 800G, 1.6T). Chinese makers supply ~two-thirds of global volume and ~60% of revenue. The US FCC is now drafting a ban on importing new Chinese transceivers.

Rob's read: "This is a choke point created by regulation, not shortage. It raises costs, delays hyperscaler buildouts, and creates upside for Western suppliers. We've chased these choke points before, memory, CPUs. Optical networking is the next one down the supply chain."

The twist that makes it messy: the dependence runs both ways. Chinese module makers rely on US DSPs (Broadcom, Marvell) and optical chips (Lumentum, Coherent, Mitsubishi), and lean on overseas buyers for 90%+ of their high-value revenue. Western suppliers can't absorb the volume for 12-24 months. Untangling it isn't quick.
