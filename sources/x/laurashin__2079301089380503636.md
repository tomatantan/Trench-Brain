"Studies show that companies that emerge from PE have a higher than average bankruptcy or financial distress rate, because they're kind of in some ways hollowed out"

Lyn Alden, co-founder of Orange Juice, on why so many companies come out of private equity worse off

"There is a really big strong demographic force of American business owners aging. Many are looking to pass on the wealth they've built, and there's a variety of options to them. One of the hardest things you can sell is a business, because of the size and uniqueness of each one, and the main option for companies in that small to medium size is private equity"

"Private equity serves a very important spot in the market. It provides liquidity for these businesses that are otherwise quite illiquid, it can be very hard to find a strategic buyer. But many would say they have a negative reputation, and at least some of it's well deserved. PE funds generally have a 10 year life. What they want to do is go in and then within a three to seven year period flip it, so they lever it up, they cut costs pretty aggressively, they try to find ways to boost the multiple, and then bring it to IPO or otherwise exit"

"They optimize for that more short term gain, potentially at the expense of the long term success of that business. Studies show that companies that emerge from PE have a higher than average bankruptcy or financial distress rate, and it's because they're kind of in some ways hollowed out"
