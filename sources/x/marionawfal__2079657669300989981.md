🇨🇳🇦🇫🇮🇷 Beijing is moving on a shut Afghan border that could hand it a road to Iran without ever touching the sea

The Wakhan Corridor was carved out at the end of the 19th century as a buffer between British India and Russian Turkestan. It touches Xinjiang for just 74 km, a strip you could cross in an hour if a road existed.

None does. The Wakhjir Pass sits at nearly 5,000 metres with no official crossing point, and the border it marks has the sharpest clock change on earth, 3.5 hours from Afghan time straight into Beijing time.

In March, China created Cenling County staring directly at that gate. Third new county in Xinjiang in just over a year, run from Kashgar, where the $60 billion CPEC begins.

Open the pass and China gets an overland road to Iran and the Gulf that never touches water. Washington is blockading Iranian shipping, and Tehran is already Afghanistan's top trading partner, pushing rail north from Herat.

The catch is that a Wakhan road competes with the corridor Beijing already paid $60 billion for through Pakistan.

Nothing has been opened. No decision, no highway, no rail over the Pamirs, just a new administrative unit facing a closed border.

Washington once asked to use this pass for troops and supplies into Afghanistan. Beijing never obliged.

It stayed shut through 20 years of American war. It may open the moment it serves China.
