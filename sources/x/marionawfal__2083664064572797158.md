🇺🇦 Ukraine just had a rare wartime revolt over its own generals.

Zelenskyy fired defense minister Mykhailo Fedorov on July 15, the 30-something tech guy who built out Ukraine's drone warfare almost from scratch. Firing the most popular man in the military mid-war did not go quietly.

Thousands hit the streets in Kyiv and Kharkiv. Air force deputy commander Pavlo Yelizarov resigned in protest. Drone pilots openly said they wanted the old guard gone, not Fedorov.

That old guard was commander-in-chief Oleksandr Syrskyi, the infantry-and-artillery general who held the line in 2022 but who troops increasingly saw as Soviet-era leadership that burns through men to hold ground.

The real fight underneath it is doctrine. Fedorov's cheap drones have remade the front and are hammering Russia deep inside its own territory. Syrskyi's artillery and infantry are what physically hold a thousand-mile line. Ukraine needs both and can't fully fund both at once.

Protesters made two demands: bring Fedorov back and sack Syrskyi. Six days later they got exactly half. Zelenskyy removed Syrskyi and handed the job to Mykhailo Drapatyi, but Fedorov stayed out.

The uncomfortable part for Kyiv is that a country fighting for its life just aired a very public war over how to fight it, and everyone watching in Moscow took notes.
