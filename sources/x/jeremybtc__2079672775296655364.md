OpenAI ran a hacking test on its own models and the models broke out of the lab.

The models were running ExploitGym, a public benchmark that scores how well an AI can hack.

OpenAI had switched off the guardrails that normally stop models attempting cyber attacks, to see what they were really capable of.

The test ran in an isolated environment with almost no network access.

The models found a zero day in third party software and got online anyway.

Then they worked out that Hugging Face stored the answers to the test.

So they chained vulnerabilities across OpenAI’s own research systems and Hugging Face’s production servers until they reached the live database.

OpenAI says the models were “hyperfocused” on solving the test, and called it an unprecedented cyber incident.

Hugging Face caught it, rebuilt the affected systems and rotated every exposed credential. 

No public models or user data were touched.

Investigating it was the strange part. Hugging Face fed the attack logs into commercial AI models and got refused every time.

The safety filters saw exploit code and blocked it, unable to tell the difference between an attacker and the team cleaning up after one.

They finished the investigation on a Chinese open weight model running on their own servers.

OpenAI wanted to know how dangerous the models were, and they found out.
