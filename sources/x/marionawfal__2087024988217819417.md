🇮🇷 Everyone’s screaming that the Iran war is about to strangle global shipping, but the actual data says the opposite.

Craig Fuller, FreightWaves CEO, dropped this on Bloomberg yesterday: international container volumes, U.S. trucking, and rail are all rising, RIGHT NOW, even with insurance and shipping costs spiking hard.

That multi-year trucking freight recession that started in 2022? Officially over since November 2025. 

The industry is finally breathing again after years of pain.

Here’s the real adaptation happening under the surface: Shippers are aggressively shifting freight from truck to rail. The rate spread between the two just hit 34%, near an all-time high. 

That kind of gap doesn’t get ignored. Railroads and intermodal players (think J.B. Hunt, Hub Group) are quietly taking share while truck capacity stays tighter, and the map itself is starting to rewrite. 

A Chinese carrier is already running an Arctic route that cuts Asia-to-Europe transit from the usual ~40 days down to 20. Melting ice + Russian icebreakers = a new corridor that completely bypasses the Strait of Hormuz. 

One more chokepoint becomes optional.

Yes, container spot rates are sitting around $7,000. Which feels elevated until you remember the COVID peak was $22,000, so there’s still massive headroom.

Even better math for the inflation worry: transportation is only about 3% of the final price of finished goods. Double freight rates tomorrow and the hit to consumers is still marginal, so most of the extra cost is getting eaten in retailer and manufacturer margins (which are at record highs) rather than passed straight through.

Ultimately, the demand is real, and the system is rerouting in real time. 

Old assumptions about permanent dependence on Hormuz and traditional sea lanes are starting to crack.

Global trade is proving once again that it can simply redraw the map instead.
