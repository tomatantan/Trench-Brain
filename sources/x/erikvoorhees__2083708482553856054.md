"But what it reminded us of is that there is simply no way to secure crypto."

This is not correct...  Sure there is no way to *perfectly* secure crypto, but there is no way to perfectly secure anything in life. There is no perfectly safe financial asset, either in its possession or its value. 

Every system to store crypto (and any other asset) has tradeoffs. They are not "equivalent" in tradeoffs, but disparate, and the specific risks vary with the specific implementation. 

Empirically, hundreds of billions of dollars of crypto has been stored securely for years. Does that mean it's perfectly secure? No, but it has been sufficiently secure. 

What's most relevant about crypto in this case is that the user can decide how they wish to implement their security and risk profile. It is broad user choice (which means user responsibility) that matters.
