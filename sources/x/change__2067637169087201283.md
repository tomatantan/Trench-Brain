On Wednesday, a horse-drawn carriage in Central Park bolted after its driver stepped away from the carriage to photograph passengers—a family visiting from India—in violation of industry protocol. The horse, who had been working in the park for just six weeks, sped off.

Eighteen-year-old Romanch Mahajan was thrown from the carriage, suffered a severe head injury, and later died. According to the New York Times, Romanch's father, Deepak Mahajan, said his son fell while trying to save his mother, Priya, who had been thrown from the carriage moments earlier. “My son, just to save his mother, he fell off,” he told the outlet.

The tragedy was the second horse-drawn carriage incident in Central Park in just eight days. On June 9, a carriage horse collapsed and died while working.

Thousands of people have signed a petition urging the New York City Council to pass Ryder’s Law, legislation that would phase out horse-drawn carriages and fund job-transition programs for drivers. Plans are underway to reintroduce the bill.
