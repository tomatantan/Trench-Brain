🇬🇧 British taxpayers may have ended up helping fund therapy for Israeli soldiers returning from Gaza.

It started with donations. The Yahalom Foundation raises money for Israeli soldiers and veterans, and British donors could send money through UK charity UK Toremet.

That’s where British taxpayers came in.

Eligible donations could get a 25% Gift Aid boost from the government. The money reportedly went toward PTSD treatment and a £5,000 “therapeutic healing journey.”

UK Toremet says the funding was only used for lawful PTSD treatment.

But here’s the irony. Britain’s charity regulator says charities can’t raise money to support soldiers in a foreign military.

So the rule is simple: don’t fund foreign soldiers. Just help pay for the therapy when they come home. Neat.
