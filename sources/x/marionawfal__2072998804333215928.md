🇮🇷 Today doesn't just mark the start of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei's funeral; it's also the anniversary of one of the darkest moments in U.S.-Iran history: the day the U.S. shot down a civilian passenger plane.

And Iran's Foreign Ministry isn't letting the U.S forget it.

On July 3, 1988, the USS Vincennes fired two missiles at Iran Air Flight 655, a scheduled commercial flight from Bandar Abbas to Dubai. 

All 290 people on board were killed, including 66 children.

The U.S. has always called it a tragic mistake rather than a deliberate attack. 

It happened during the Iran-Iraq War, with the Vincennes in the middle of a skirmish with Iranian gunboats in the Strait of Hormuz. 

The crew misidentified the climbing Airbus A300 as an Iranian F-14 in an attack dive, despite the plane flying its assigned civilian corridor.

Washington never formally apologized or admitted legal liability. 

In 1996 it agreed to pay Iran roughly $61.8 million in compensation to the victims' families, without conceding wrongdoing.

Iran also points to the fact that the Vincennes commander, Captain Will Rogers III, received the Legion of Merit in 1990. 

The U.S. says that was a routine end-of-tour award for his full command period, not a reward for the shootdown, but the timing drew heavy criticism at home and abroad.

38 years on, Tehran calls it an unforgivable crime it will never forget. 

For much of the world it remains a case study in how quickly things go catastrophically wrong when warships and civilian jets share the same sky.
