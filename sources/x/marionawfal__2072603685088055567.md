🇮🇱🇵🇸 It's been 1,000 days since the October 7, 2023 attacks, and both sides of the line marked it very differently today.

In Israel, commemorations ran across the country: marches, protests, a minute of silence, organized by the October Council of bereaved families, survivors, and former captives. 

Some carried a banner reading "1,000 days of abandonment, neglect, cover-up and failure." 

Others waved yellow flags, blocked traffic, and tried to disrupt access to the Knesset. 

More demonstrations are set for later in Tel Aviv, outside the Defense Ministry and at Hostages Square.

In Gaza, the same 1,000 days have meant what many consider a genocidal war. 

Israeli forces have killed at least 73,066 Palestinians and wounded 173,514, according to Gaza's health ministry.

Strikes have eased since the October 2025 ceasefire but continue almost daily, with 1,053 killed since then, more than 350 of them women and children. 

Every border crossing remains closed or tightly restricted, 17 hospitals are still out of service for lack of supplies, and famine conditions were declared in Gaza City last August.

COGAT, the Israeli body overseeing civilian affairs in Gaza, says the food it lets in "far exceed the nutritional needs" of the population.

The same clock started for both on October 7. It has not been measuring the same thing since.
