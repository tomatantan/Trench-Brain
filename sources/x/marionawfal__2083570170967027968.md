🇺🇸🇮🇷 Trump may be preparing to make the biggest gamble of his presidency.

Iranian analyst Nima Rostami Alkhorshid says reports of U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran's energy infrastructure would mark a major escalation, one that Tehran has spent years preparing for.

Start with the targets.

The reported plan isn't just to hit missile launchers or military sites.

It's to strike the infrastructure that powers Iran's economy, while degrading its ability to retaliate over a campaign that could last days or even weeks.

Nima believes Iran's response has already been mapped out.

Israel would remain the primary target, but Gulf oil facilities, power infrastructure and American military assets across the region would also come into play.

Not because Tehran wants a wider war.

Because it wants Washington to understand that attacks on Iran's economy will immediately become attacks on the region's economy.

That brings him to the Strait of Hormuz.

Nima argues the dispute isn't really about charging ships a transit fee. It's all about security.

Iran wants control over what enters the Gulf after watching weapons, missiles and military equipment flow through the region during the conflict. In his view, controlling inbound traffic is now seen in Tehran as essential to preventing another war.

He believes it's a struggle over whether the Middle East remains under American dominance or moves toward a new regional balance of power.

And if neither side is willing to concede that question, the military campaign may last far longer than anyone currently expects.
