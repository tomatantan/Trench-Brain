🇺🇸 A tiny group of stocks is now running the entire U.S. market.

Nvidia alone is responsible for 3.0% of all year-to-date notional trading volume.

Micron sits right behind at 2.8%,  and Tesla at 2.1%

Add up the top 10 and you’re looking at 16.4% of every dollar that changes hands across the whole market.

That’s extreme concentration.

But the part most people miss: heavy volume does not equal deep liquidity.

Tesla, Microsoft, and Alphabet all show smaller average order sizes sitting at the best bid and offer than a typical index stock, so the market is pouring massive activity into a handful of names while the actual depth underneath some of them remains relatively thin.

This is what a top-heavy market looks like in real time: A few stocks can move the entire line, and when liquidity is thinner than the volume suggests, the risk of sharp, cascading swings rises fast.

The market isn’t as deep as the numbers make it feel.
