🇺🇸🇮🇱 Kushner is flying back into a messy regional chessboard next week.

The schedule is Israel first, then Cairo for talks with Egyptian, Qatari and Turkish mediators.

The goal remains the same Trump plan floated months ago: Hamas disarmament, a phased Israeli withdrawal, an international stabilization force, and a new technocratic Palestinian administration running Gaza.

Washington says the sides agree on the end goal, although Israel says the details are a non-starter.

Netanyahu has already rejected the plan publicly. Israeli officials see any withdrawal while Hamas still has tunnels, rockets or organized fighters as repeating past mistakes.

With elections coming in October, flexibility is even harder. Any deal that looks like rewarding Hamas or leaving it with residual capability is political poison.

Meanwhile, the wider map keeps shifting.

Saudi, Pakistan and Turkey are deepening defense coordination under the Mecca framework, with Egypt moving closer to that conversation. All four are now part of the broader regional diplomacy.

That creates an awkward overlap. Washington is leaning on traditional mediators as a parallel security architecture emerges that is less dependent on the U.S. and more focused on collective defense.

Kushner’s trip is an attempt to close those gaps before the calendar runs out.

On paper, the plan looks clean. On the ground, it runs into Israeli red lines, Hamas’s refusal to fully disarm first, and a region quietly building alternative power centers.

Whether next week brings real movement or just another round of carefully worded statements will show how much leverage anyone still has.
