🇮🇷🇴🇲 Iran wants to turn the world's most important oil chokepoint into a tollbooth, and the U.S. and Gulf states just said no.

Tehran first welcomed Oman's plan to jointly manage the Strait of Hormuz, then showed up with a wish list. It now wants the right to charge shipping fees on every vessel passing through, possibly split with Oman.

And that's just the start of the wish list: guarantees against fresh attacks, an end to the U.S. naval blockade on its ports, and while they're at it, relief from oil sanctions too.

Regional officials say the U.S. and Gulf governments flatly rejected the fee grab. What they want back is a guarantee that Iran and its proxies won't threaten their territory.

The real fight is over who gets to write the rules for a strait that carries roughly 20% of the world's oil, and whether passing through it now comes with a price tag.
