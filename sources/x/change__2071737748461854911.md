For years, shelters across California paid Miranda's Rescue, a self-described "no-kill" nonprofit in Fortuna, to take in dogs they could no longer accommodate. Now, the rescue is at the center of a scandal that has sent shockwaves through animal-loving communities across the country.

The Humboldt County Sheriff’s Office opened an investigation last April after local animal advocates captured trail camera footage that appeared to show dogs being buried on the property. A multi-day excavation concluded last week and recovered the remains of 117 dogs. Forensic veterinarians examining the site found evidence of bullet fragments in many of the remains. Investigators say at least 700 other rescues who had been transferred to the shelter remain unaccounted for.

Authorities allege that Miranda intentionally killed dogs with a firearm to make room for new arrivals while continuing to collect intake fees. Miranda has denied the allegations, and the investigation remains ongoing.

More than 5,000 people have signed a petition calling for everyone responsible for the deaths of these dogs to be held accountable.
