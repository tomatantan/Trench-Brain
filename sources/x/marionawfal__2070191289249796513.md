After 8 straight years of decline, the global democracy recession may finally be ending.

The EIU's latest Democracy Index found nearly three-quarters of countries either improved or held steady over the past year, the first real break in the slide since it started.

Norway is still the world's most democratic country, a spot it's held for 16 years now, with New Zealand second and the Nordics rounding out the top.

Most of the gains came from the middle of the pack, with Latin America the most improved region after 9 years of decline, helped by more people getting involved in politics.

The U.S. slipped a few spots to 34th, staying in the "flawed democracy" group it's been in since 2016.

Democracy didn't bounce back, it just stopped falling, which counts for something after nearly a decade.

Source: The Economist, EIU / Writer: Julie
