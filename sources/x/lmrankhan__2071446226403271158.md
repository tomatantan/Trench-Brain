During mentorship, I ask founders a simple question:

Why did you choose this idea?

Sometimes, I get founders who struggle to answer it and I typically push back hard. 

When that happens they think the idea is wrong or won't work, so they decide to pivot. I let them.

What's interesting is that exploring a new direction usually forces them to talk to more users, gather better data, and uncover better insights. Through that process, they end up discovering why they chose the original idea in the first place and end up pivoting back.

I'm completely fine with that. In fact I think it's a great outcome. Because now they know exactly who they're building for, what problem they're solving, and why the market cares. 

The lesson is if your company is built on a single core premise, you should understand every detail behind it. You should be teaching me the reasons why I should care about this problem.

Why this problem? Why now? Why do users care? Why is the market pulling in this direction?

If you can't answer those questions, you need to get out of the building, talk to more people, and build real conviction via customer insights. Otherwise, you're building in the dark.
