🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿🇮🇪🇦🇷 Why are so many Irish and Scots backing Argentina in today’s match against England?

It goes back further than the Falklands, and it's funnier than most people realize. 

Scotland vs England is the oldest international fixture in football, dating back to 1872, and Scots have been rooting against their southern neighbors on principle ever since. 

They call it "anyone but England," ABE for short, and it's been in full force today with Argentina jerseys spotted from Glasgow to Perth and bagpipers playing Argentina's own anthem in the streets.

Some of the connections people are digging up are wild. Argentina's first football league was founded in 1893 by a Glaswegian schoolteacher named Alexander Watson Hutton, still remembered there as the father of Argentine football. 

Diego Maradona scored his first international goal as an 18 year old at Hampden Park in Scotland. Even Alexis Mac Allister's surname is being treated like proof of Scottish blood running through this Argentina squad.

Then there's the part that makes zero sense on paper. Scots Guards died fighting Argentina at the Battle of Mount Tumbledown during the Falklands War back in 1982, and some of their own countrymen are still choosing to cheer for the other side 44 years later just to needle England.

Ireland's version of this story has a real hero behind it. 

William Brown was born in Foxford, County Mayo in 1777, and after his family fell into hardship in America he ended up in Buenos Aires, where he built Argentina's navy from scratch and helped win its independence from Spain. 

He's a bigger deal in Argentina than he ever was in Ireland, with over a thousand streets, hundreds of statues, and even football clubs carrying his name. The Irish rebel band the Wolfe Tones even wrote a song about him in 1982, timed as a direct jab at the British during the Falklands War.

Two different countries, one shared hobby: rooting against England.
