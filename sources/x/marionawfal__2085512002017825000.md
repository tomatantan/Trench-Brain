One proposal yanked the oil and gold markets in two directions at once.

Iran’s parliament is reviewing a draft bill that would ban U.S., Israeli, and other “hostile” vessels from the Strait of Hormuz, and fine violators up to 20% of a ship’s cargo value. But it’s still under expert review, so it's not law yet.

Regardless, oil jumped more than $3 a barrel on the news, and gold briefly touched a seven-week high before giving those gains right back and finishing steady.

That single waterway moves about a fifth of the world’s oil. Even the threat of tighter controls is enough to push energy prices higher, revive inflation fears, and force traders to rethink safe-haven demand.

Higher crude feeds into consumer prices, and that raises the odds of tighter monetary policy. Gold hates that environment, so you get this exact reaction: energy rallies, precious metals hesitate, and volatility spikes across the board.

Markets are still hanging on every headline from the region until it’s clear whether this bill moves forward, or dies in committee... the swinging isn’t over.
