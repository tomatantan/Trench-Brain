🇨🇴 A 7.4-magnitude earthquake ripped through Colombia yesterday, centered in San José del Palmar in the western Chocó region.

More than 3,000 people are now reported missing.

Local mayors and governors across Cali, Pereira, Chocó and Manizales say at least 179 have died. The federal government's own count still sits at 111, last updated in the hours right after the quake hit.

Around 1,600 buildings have collapsed or been damaged.

Newly inaugurated President Abelardo De la Espriella rushed to Chocó's capital Quibdó the night of the quake and mobilized the entire military and police apparatus, sending in engineers, rescue teams and search dogs.

"Chocó will never again be the land of the forgotten" 

The Trump administration is stepping in too, pledging $15.5 million for emergency shelter, food, protection and earthquake assessments, with the State Department saying they're standing ready to support De la Espriella's government as they assess the damage.

More than 1,400 families have already been allocated emergency aid, and the injured are being transferred to Medellín for hospital care.

A brand new president, barely days into office, facing a disaster of this scale right out of the gate.
