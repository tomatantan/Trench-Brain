A principal in Pennsylvania left a school therapy dog named Callie in a hot car for nearly eight hours on June 11, which proved to be fatal, according to disciplinary charges the Warwick School Board later brought against her. A hearing to decide whether she'll be fired was set for this week before the district postponed it, with no new date announced.

While the Lititz Police Department recommended criminal charges, the Lancaster County District Attorney's office decided not to prosecute, concluding the evidence didn't meet the legal standard for intent or recklessness required under Pennsylvania's animal cruelty law. "The law simply does not punish forgetting," District Attorney Heather Adams said. 

Thousands have signed a petition asking the DA to reconsider whether Pennsylvania's animal neglect law applies to Callie's death. The petition also calls on the school district to implement a set of safeguards called "Callie's Code" to prevent future harm to any facility animals.
