🇮🇳🇺🇸🇨🇳 India is playing both America and China... and winning.

Ian Bremmer called it the strongest geopolitical position of any middle power on the planet, not because India is the biggest military or the richest economy, but because it refuses to pick a side and still gets invited to every table.

Look at the map, India sits in the Quad with the U.S., Japan, and Australia, tightening security ties, sharing intel, buying weapons. 

At the same time it keeps a stable (if cool) relationship with China, its biggest trading partner, and leads the Global South in a way Beijing never could. 

Most developing countries trust New Delhi more than they trust the Chinese creditor, and that’s the pivot-state advantage: Economic scale big enough to matter, geography that gives it insulation, diplomatic room that smaller countries can only dream of. 

While Europe frets about American reliability and everyone else gets squeezed, India deepens partnerships without locking itself into anyone’s dependency.

In a G-zero world, where no single power is writing the rules anymore, this flexibility is pure power. 

Influence increasingly belongs to the countries that can work with competing giants and still walk away independent. India is already doing it, and the gap between it and everyone else is only getting wider.
