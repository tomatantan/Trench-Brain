I think people misunderstand why prop firms became so big.

It's not because traders believe they'll definitely make it this time.

It's because once you've traded with $10,000, $50,000, or $100,000, your brain simply stops respecting a $100 account.

You can't lose six figures, spend years in the market, and suddenly go back to trading with $100 of your own money. Mentally, it doesn't work.

That's why people pay $50-$100 for prop firm challenges.

Not because they need $100.

Because they need access to the level of capital their brain is already calibrated to.

Crypto exchanges are making the same mistake.

They spend millions acquiring new users while ignoring the graveyard of traders who once traded size, got wiped out, and disappeared.

What if exchanges created a Trader Revival Program?

"Welcome back. Here's your second chance." From the fees you once paid.

Not charity.

Not an airdrop.

Just a bridge back to the level of capital and activity the trader once belonged to.

The industry doesn't have a user acquisition problem. It has a trader resurrection problem.

Most traders didn't leave because they stopped loving the markets. They left because they could no longer afford to play the game they once belonged to.

So Dear exchanges think about it.
