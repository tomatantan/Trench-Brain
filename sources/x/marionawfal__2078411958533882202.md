AI may be changing the national security landscape for the worse. 

As wars and tensions intensify across Europe, the Middle East, and the Indo-Pacific, even basic AI tools have been found to make terror groups more capable than ever before.

Researchers at the University of Cambridge found Boko Haram factions are using multiple AI models to support operational planning.

A UN-supported Tech Against Terrorism study meanwhile found that nearly one-third of extremist prompts generate useful outputs despite existing safeguards.

The biggest change is that AI raises the baseline. 

Instead of spending weeks searching forums, manuals, or finding experienced mentors, chatbots can instantly help users refine plans, solve operational problems, and adapt tactics to real-world conditions.

And simply using AI to counteract that is no simple task due to the first mover advantage. 

An attacker only needs one successful use case, while governments must defend against every possible attack. 

That asymmetry is pushing the world into a new era of elevated security risks: one where AI is significantly increasing the capabilities of even lone wolf attackers as governments continue to reassure the public that the threat remains under control.
