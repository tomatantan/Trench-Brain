🇩🇪🇪🇺 A drought is about to do to Europe what the Iran war couldn't

Europe spent months bracing for oil chaos out of the Gulf, watching Hormuz, the Black Sea, and the Red Sea. Now a dry summer is doing what all those threats couldn't.

The Rhine, the 800-mile river running through the industrial heart of Europe, is drying up. It carries the fuel, chemicals, and steel that feed factories across Switzerland, Germany, and France, and moves around 80% of everything shipped on German rivers. BASF puts it plainly: without the Rhine, industry in western Europe would collapse.

At Kaub, the key chokepoint near Frankfurt, the water just dropped to 28 centimeters, down from a normal 200-plus. This week it's forecast to hit 24, the lowest since Germany began keeping records in 1990. At that depth barges can barely carry anything, and freight costs from Rotterdam have already nearly tripled in a month.

The knock-on is already starting. Switzerland may have to tap its strategic oil reserves within weeks, German chemical and steel plants could cut output, and parts of Germany are facing possible fuel shortages, all while businesses are still running lean on the pricey fuel the Iran war left behind.

The hardest part is the timing. The Rhine's driest months, August and September, haven't even arrived yet.

Europe spent all year braced for a war over oil, and the real threat is turning out to be the weather. 

You can sign a ceasefire with Iran. There's no signing one with the sky.
