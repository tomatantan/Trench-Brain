Don't do this. 

If you're doing well, I want to hear about it so I can help amplify your success.

If you're struggling (which is typical), I want to know about it. If I know what you're trying, I might have ideas or intros that can help. But most of all, even if you fail, you want your investors and angels to remember who you are, to understand your journey, and to come away with the impression that you tried like hell to succeed. 

There is little downside to being top of mind for your investors.

Most startups fail, that's normal. But even among startups that don't make it, they don't all come out with the positive regard from their investors.

In 150+ investments, never complained that a startup communicated too much.
