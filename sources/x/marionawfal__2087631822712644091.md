🚨🇺🇸🇮🇷 Sailors on the USS Abraham Lincoln have tried to JUMP overboard after 250 days at sea

-Navy Times and Stars and Stripes report multiple attempts by crew members to go overboard as conditions and mental strain aboard the carrier reach a breaking point

-The roughly 5,000 sailors and Marines are in their ninth month at sea and have gone 250 consecutive days without making land, a record for a modern aircraft carrier

-They have had two days on land in that entire span, Guam in December and Oman in July, and the ship is in its fifth month of combat operations against Iran

-The Lincoln left San Diego on November 21 bound for the Pacific, was rerouted to the Middle East when the war began, and has had its return pushed back twice since a scheduled May end date

-About 200 family members confronted Navy leadership at a San Diego town hall last Thursday, including acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao, with one spouse telling him leadership had broken trust with the families

-Annabelle Loma told Navy Times her husband attempted to go overboard after his time at sea kept getting extended, saying he fears a dishonorable discharge will end a 13 year career over BURNOUT

-Representative Mike Levin described moldy showers, broken toilets, laundry down for weeks, long stretches without hot water, a meal of half a cup of rice and two tortillas, and no soap, deodorant or toothpaste

-Hegseth dismissed similar complaints in April as fake news, and a Navy official now says the service has not identified an increase in reported suicidal ideation or attempts aboard

-This lands days after Bloomberg reported that as many as five people who worked in or with U.S. Cyber Command died by suicide between early June and early July, a stretch the command formally classified as a suicide cluster

-Causes there remain under investigation, though the deaths revived long standing concerns about staffing shortages and a workload driven up by the Iran war, with General Joshua Rudd having warned lawmakers that burnout and force degradation will increase without more funding for mental health support
