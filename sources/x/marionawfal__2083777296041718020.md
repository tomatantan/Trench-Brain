🇬🇧 BP is exiting the UK North Sea after more than 60 years.

The company is selling its entire business there: assets that still produce around 117,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day, roughly 5% of BP’s global output.

On paper it’s a portfolio cleanup. In reality it’s a verdict on mature basins.

The UK North Sea has gone from 4.5 million barrels a day in 2000 to about 1 million today. 

Natural decline is accelerating, operating costs keep climbing, and the fiscal environment has grown heavier. 

Every extra barrel now demands more capital for smaller returns.

BP’s move fits a clear pattern: majors are no longer willing to chase volume for its own sake. They’re concentrating capital on lower-cost, higher-margin barrels and walking away from legacy production that no longer clears the hurdle rate.

The bigger issue is replacement.

If investment keeps leaving aging basins faster than new supply comes online, the gap shows up in global balances, energy security calculations, and future price volatility.
