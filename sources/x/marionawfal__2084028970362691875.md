🇪🇺🇪🇸 The migrant invasion in Ceuta this week laid bare just how brittle the whole EU border setup really is.

This tiny Spanish enclave (pop ~84k) is one of only 2 land borders the EU has with Africa. 

When 60-70K people swam around the breakwater or pushed through in a day, local police were instantly outnumbered.

Reception centres hit 1,600-2,400% capacity, and Spain had to call in the army. 

Most ALLEGEDLY went back within 48h, but the damage was done.

The real fragility sits deeper:

- Outsourced control: Europe relies on Morocco to police the fence. One political wobble, rumour, or court ruling and the floodgates open. It's the same playbooke we've seen time and again since 2021.

- Schengen’s weak link: Free movement inside the EU only works if the outer edge holds. Ceuta has a special status, you still need ID checks to reach mainland Spain, but the perception that “you’re in Europe” is enough to trigger rushes.

- Legal and capacity gaps: Frontex can offer help, but national governments still run the show. A few dozen officers can’t hold a 8 km frontier against tens of thousands.

The EU built a system that assumes the periphery will always hold and neighbours will always cooperate. 

Ceuta shows what happens when either assumption cracks: military deployment, diplomatic fights, and the free-movement dream starts looking like a nightmare. 

One small enclave can force the entire architecture to strain.
