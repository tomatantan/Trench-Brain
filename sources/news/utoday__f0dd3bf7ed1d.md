Bitcoin breaking the key barrier Bearish tendencies on the market Advertisement As Bitcoin seeks to stabilize above $60,000, its on-chain activity has returned to levels last observed during the 2018&ndash;2019 bear market transition, drawing a significant historical parallel. Bitcoin active addresses are the source of the crucial signal. The 30-day EMA of active addresses recently hit a low of about 609,700, while the 100-day EMA reached about 622,000, according to the data provided.&nbsp; Bitcoin breaking the key barrier At this point, both averages began to rise. This puts network activity in the vicinity of the 600,000-address range, which signaled a significant change during the 2018&ndash;2019 cycle. At that time, the 100-day EMA bottomed at about 605,400 active addresses, while the 30-day EMA dropped to about 570,700. Before finally going into a recovery phase, Bitcoin itself reached about $3,200.&nbsp; BTC/USDT Chart by TradingView As a result, even though the price and general market structure of Bitcoin are entirely different, the current numbers are remarkably close in terms of network participation. The bottoming argument receives additional support from the price chart. Additionally, the asset has recovered its shorter moving averages; the chart indicates support at $64,247 and $63,346. The momentum is now marginally on the bullish side of neutral, as the RSI has recovered to roughly 54.6. The general trend has not yet changed, though.&nbsp; Advertisement Bearish tendencies on the market The larger moving averages of $66,819 and $72,193 are still above Bitcoin. The latter is still falling, indicating that there is still significant overhead resistance for BTC . The short-term structure would be strengthened by a move through $66,800, and a break above $72,000 would offer far more convincing proof that the larger downtrend is losing its grip. Additionally, care should be taken when comparing active addresses.&nbsp; Similar network activity does not imply that Bitcoin will rebound as it did in 2019. Transaction behavior, exchange activity, batching, and more general changes in the way the Bitcoin network is used can all affect active addresses. The indicator's direction is what counts. The fact that both active-address EMAs are rising after entering historically low territory may indicate that the decline in network participation has stopped.&nbsp; As of right now, the data suggests a potential bottoming structure rather than a standalone buy signal.
