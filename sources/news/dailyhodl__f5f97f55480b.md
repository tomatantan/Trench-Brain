The agreement establishes a $31.5 million fund to compensate affected individuals, reports USA Today. The breaches occurred in January 2021 and December 2021, exposing personal information belonging to approximately 2.19 million people nationwide, including about 364,000 California residents. Eligible class members can seek reimbursement of up to $25,000 for documented losses such as fraud or identity theft costs. Those without specific losses may receive a residual cash payment estimated around $60, with the possibility of higher amounts depending on claims volume. Participants also qualify for three years of credit monitoring services along with up to $1 million in identity theft insurance coverage. California residents stand to receive an additional statutory payment of up to $100 under the terms. The settlement stems from the case Angus et al. v. Flagstar Bank, N.A., filed in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan. Flagstar has denied any wrongdoing but agreed to the resolution to avoid prolonged litigation costs. Claims must be submitted by August 11, 2026, via the official settlement website at flagstarsettlement.com. A final fairness hearing is scheduled for October 2026, after which approved payments would begin distribution. The settlement class members will be able to get the payments via various methods including checks and payment apps such as PayPal, Venmo or Zelle.
