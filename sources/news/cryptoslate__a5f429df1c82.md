Make CryptoSlate preferred on Share Share A warning posted on X on Aug. 5 urged Bitcoin users to avoid entering their own wallet addresses into public block explorers. It alleged that explorers log visitors' internet protocol addresses, sell the information to Chainalysis, and ultimately expose it to government agencies. Public block explorers allow anyone to enter an address or transaction identifier to retrieve records from a blockchain. Like other websites, a hosted explorer receives connection details and the requested page. When that request includes a public blockchain address , the operator may technically be able to associate an internet connection with interest in the address. That creates a layer of off-chain metadata around a public on-chain record. Its privacy significance depends on whether the operator joins an IP address to a query, how long it retains the record, and who can receive it. Published policies from WalletExplorer, mempool.space, Blockchair, Etherscan , Blockchain.com and OKLink describe materially different practices. WalletExplorer identifies itself as a Chainalysis website and explicitly describes sharing visitor information within the company. The other policies range from short webserver-log schedules to purpose-based retention. Together, they support a focused conclusion: hosted explorers can collect connection and request metadata, while linkage, duration and disclosure vary by operator. WalletExplorer documents sharing within Chainalysis WalletExplorer's privacy notice says the site's servers automatically record the IP address of the requesting user and the requested URL, which may contain a Wallet Name, Wallet ID, or Bitcoin address. The notice also says that blockchain information and visitor information are shared with other Chainalysis business lines, making this a disclosed flow within the corporate operator. Service providers may also process or access the data to perform functions on Chainalysis' behalf. Its legal disclosure language covers law enforcement requests, court orders, and other legal processes. That provision establishes a route for disclosure under specified conditions and leaves the visitor-log retention period without a numeric limit. The notice was last updated Oct. 14, 2021, and remains publicly accessible as the service's published privacy statement in August 2026. In 2021, CoinDesk reported that it had authenticated leaked documents describing the use of WalletExplorer IP data associated with Bitcoin addresses to generate leads for law enforcement. The episode involved WalletExplorer and Chainalysis at that time. A hosting, analytics or security provider processing data for another explorer occupies a different role, and a processor clause alone is insufficient to establish a sale of lookup records. Explorer policies vary on linkage, retention and disclosure The policies differ in whether, and how specifically, they describe joining an IP address to an address query. They also use different retention clocks and definitions.
