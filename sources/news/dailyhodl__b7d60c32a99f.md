The US national debt has hit more than $39 trillion, with much of it originally sold when yields were below 2% and now must be renewed amid 10-year rates near 4.7%, reports 24/7 Wall St . As older Treasury securities issued at lower interest rates mature, the government must refinance them by issuing new debt at today&#8217;s higher market rates, increasing the cost of servicing the national debt. Data shows that the US government paid $857 billion from October 2025 to June 2026 to cover the net interest on its public debt. The 10-year rates are now hovering at the highest level since before the 2007 financial crisis. Meanwhile, the 30-year reached 5.182%, and the 2-year stood at 4.343%. Debt has surged by about $16 trillion since 2020 and stands on track to surpass $50 trillion before the end of the decade. Rising yields on long-dated bonds come as Brent crude prices have climbed above $100 per barrel and weekly jobless claims dropped to 187,000 against expectations of 212,000, prompting some Federal Reserve officials to consider a rate increase this year. The federal funds rate upper bound has remained steady at 3.75% since late 2025. Higher yields are pushing up costs for mortgages, auto loans and business borrowing across the economy. Corporate profit growth slowed markedly from 6% to 1.7% quarter over quarter, suggesting that cumulative rate pressure is starting to take its toll. These pressures coincide with a broader economic backdrop where debt service expenses consume a larger share of federal outlays than in prior decades.
