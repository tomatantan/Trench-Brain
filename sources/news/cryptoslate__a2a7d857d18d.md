May 25, 2026 &middot; Gino Matos The dispute combines a jurisdictional test with a commercial fight over institutional Bitcoin options. CME operates the largest US federally regulated crypto derivatives franchise under Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) oversight, while Nasdaq is seeking to offer comparable direct Bitcoin-options exposure through the securities market. Nasdaq targets a market built under CFTC rules Nasdaq’s proposal would bring direct Bitcoin index options into the brokerage and clearing infrastructure investors already use for stocks and ETF options. QBTC would be a cash-settled contract exercisable at expiration, with intraday pricing linked to the CME CF Bitcoin Real Time Index and final settlement based on the New York variant of the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate. Its multiplier would give each contract reference exposure roughly equal to one Bitcoin. That route places Nasdaq closer to a market CME has developed through CFTC-regulated futures and options. The timing is significant because growing institutional participation has accelerated demand for Bitcoin options used to hedge exposure, trade volatility and generate yield. Jun 21, 2026 &middot; Andjela Radmilac CME said in February that its broader cryptocurrency derivatives suite averaged 407,200 contracts per day in 2026, up 46% from a year earlier, while average daily open interest reached 335,400 contracts. Meanwhile, Nasdaq’s proposal also relies on the CME CF benchmark family used across CME’s Bitcoin products. The relationship gives CME a direct commercial interest in how similar exposure reaches investors and which regulatory obligations apply to the exchanges offering it. CME used the commodities framework when it certified options tied directly to the BRRNY benchmark in October 2025. Those financially settled swaps represented 0.01 Bitcoin per contract and were designated for trading through CME Globex and clearing by CME Clearing under CFTC rules. QBTC would carry approximately 100 times that reference exposure through an SEC-regulated exchange. CME says Nasdaq would gain permission to offer an economically comparable product without registering as a designated contract market or swap execution facility. The product would also be cleared by the Options Clearing Corporation (OCC) in its SEC-regulated capacity rather than under the full derivatives clearing framework CME follows. Its petition describes the approval as a direct competitive injury and warns that similar exemptions could raise compliance costs for CME exchanges and clearing operations. CME has not quantified any potential loss of trading volume, fees, or market share. CME challenges the bridge behind Nasdaq’s approval That commercial overlap sits behind CME’s challenge to the legal mechanism SEC staff constructed for QBTC. CME argues that Bitcoin is a non-security commodity, making a direct option on its price a commodity-option swap under the Commodity Exchange Act. Such contracts fall within the CFTC’s exclusive jurisdiction and ordinarily must trade on CFTC-regulated venues, its petition said. SEC staff acknowledged that QBTC could not launch without CFTC relief. However, the May approval said Exchange Act Section 3B could deem the contracts securities if the CFTC granted an exemption conditioned on concurrent SEC jurisdiction. The staff relied partly on Section 717 of the Dodd-Frank Act, which allows concurrent oversight of certain derivatives. It said the language applies to any qualifying contract receiving the specified CFTC exemption and is not restricted to products combining obvious securities and derivatives features.
