By James Van Straten , Stephen Alpher | Edited by Stephen Alpher Updated Aug 7, 2026, 12:34 p.m. Published Aug 7, 2026, 12:31 p.m. 1 min read Make preferred on Share Share this article Copy link X icon X (Twitter) LinkedIn Facebook Email Make preferred on The U.S. labor market showed weakness for the second consecutive month in July, possibly giving the Federal Reserve room to hold rates in place despite high inflation. According to the government’s Nonfarm Payrolls Report released Friday morning, the U.S. lost 23,000 jobs last month. That was far below the consensus expectation of a gain of 80,000 jobs, and down from June’s 20,000 (revised from an originally reported 57,000). The last negative jobs print was in February, when the U.S. lost 156,000 jobs. The unemployment rate dipped to 4.1%, compared with the expected 4.2% and June’s 4.2%. Ahead of this morning’s data, markets were split on whether the Fed would hike rates at its next policy meeting in September. According to CME FedWatch , interest rate traders were pricing in a 55% chance the U.S. central bank would tighten next month.
